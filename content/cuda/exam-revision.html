Lesson 2 of 8

Formula sheet 1: speed and limits

Speedup and efficiencyExam favourite
S = Told ÷ Tnew,   E = S ÷ N
Count copy in, kernel and copy out in Tnew.
Amdahl (fixed problem)Exam favourite
S(N) = 1 ÷ ((1 − P) + P ÷ N),   ceiling 1 ÷ (1 − P)
P is the parallel fraction of the old time.
Gustafson (problem grows)
S(N) = (1 − P) + P N
Weak scaling: the parallel work grows with N.
Peak FLOP/sExam favourite
peak = SMs × cores per SM × 2 × clock
The 2 is one FMA per core per cycle.
Little's law
in flight = latency × throughput
Warps needed = in flight ÷ 32. Bytes in flight = bandwidth × latency.
Arithmetic intensityExam favourite
AI = FLOPs ÷ bytes moved
Count bytes read and written.
RooflineExam favourite
attainable = min(peak, AI × bandwidth),   ridge = peak ÷ bandwidth
Left of the ridge: memory bound. Right: compute bound.
Time bound
t ≥ max(FLOPs ÷ peak, bytes ÷ bandwidth)
The larger term tells you what to fix.
Lesson 3 of 8

Formula sheet 2: threads and memory

Global index and blocksExam favourite
i = blockIdx.x × blockDim.x + threadIdx.x,   blocks = ⌈N ÷ B⌉
Guard with i < N. Idle threads = blocks × B − N.
Going backwards
block = ⌊g ÷ (BxBy)⌋, x = r mod Bx, y = ⌊r ÷ Bx⌋
r is the leftover g − block × BxBy.
WarpsExam favourite
warps per block = ⌈B ÷ 32⌉,   lane = t mod 32
t = x + y Bx + z BxBy, x moves fastest.
OccupancyExam favourite
blocks per SM = min(threads, blocks, registers, shared memory limits)
Occupancy = active warps ÷ max warps per SM.
Coalescing
sectors per warp load = distinct 32 byte pieces touched
Floats: stride 1 gives 4 sectors, stride 8 or more gives 32.
Bank conflictsExam favourite
degree = gcd(stride, 32)
Same word for all lanes is a broadcast. Pad rows to 33.
Matmul loadsExam favourite
naive 2N³,   tiled 2N³ ÷ T
Per thread 2N against 2N ÷ T. Tile AI in FP32 is T ÷ 4.
Halo tiles
loads per output = (B + 2R)d ÷ Bd
d is the number of dimensions, R the radius.
Lesson 4 of 8

Formula sheet 3: patterns, concurrency and number formats

Reduction treeExam favourite
log₂ B levels, B − 1 additions, 1 + log₂ B barriers
One atomic per block joins the blocks.
ScansExam favourite
Hillis and Steele n log₂ n − (n − 1) adds in log₂ n steps, Blelloch 2(n − 1) adds in 2 log₂ n steps
Blelloch does less work, Hillis and Steele has fewer steps.
Divergence
efficiency = busy lane ticks ÷ (32 × total ticks)
Paths inside one warp run one after the other.
PipelineExam favourite
T = (a + b + c) + (n − 1) × max(a, b, c)
Equal stages give speedup 3n ÷ (n + 2).
Waves
waves = ⌈blocks ÷ (SMs × blocks per SM)⌉
A last wave that is almost empty wastes most of the GPU.
Float valueExam favourite
x = (−1)s × 1.m × 2e − bias,   bias = 2E − 1 − 1
FP32 1/8/23, FP16 1/5/10, BF16 1/8/7, TF32 1/8/10.
Symmetric INT8
s = max|x| ÷ 127,   q = round(x ÷ s)
Error at most s ÷ 2 unless a value is clipped.
Interleavings
(n s)! ÷ (s!)n
n threads with s steps each. Two threads of 3 steps: 20 orders.

Lesson 6 of 8

The traps that cost marks

TrapDo this instead
Counting threads when the question asks for warpsDivide by 32 and round up per block, then multiply by blocks.
Loads per thread when the question says per blockMultiply by threads per block. Quiz 1 question 2: 256 × 2048 = 524,288.
Forgetting the write in bytes movedSAXPY reads x and y and writes y: 12 bytes per element.
Taking FMA as 1 FLOPOne FMA is 2 FLOPs, a multiply and an add.
Putting __syncthreads inside a divergent ifEvery thread of the block must reach every barrier.
Treating occupancy as the goalEnough warps to hide latency is enough. Measure, do not maximise.
Reading GB as 230 bytes in bandwidthBandwidth uses 109 bytes per GB.
Forgetting the reserved 1 KB of shared memory per blockOn CC 8.x, blocks = ⌊pool ÷ (bytes + 1 KB)⌋.
Thinking divergence happens across warpsDifferent warps may take different paths for free. Only lanes inside one warp diverge.
Amdahl with N where the question gives a speedup of a partUse 1 ÷ ((1 − p) + p ÷ s) with s the speedup of that part.
Lesson 7 of 8

Mixed problems with fresh numbers

Each problem joins two or more chapters. The numbers are new, so you cannot remember the answer, you have to work it out.

Problem A. A T4 has 40 SMs with 64 FP32 cores each at 1.59 GHz and 320 GB/s. A 3 × 3 blur does 9 multiplies and 8 adds per pixel. Is it memory or compute bound, without and with perfect reuse of input pixels?
  1. Peak = 40 × 64 × 2 × 1.59 = 8140.8 GFLOP/s. Ridge = 8140.8 ÷ 320 = 25.44 FLOP/byte.
  2. Without reuse each pixel reads 9 floats and writes 1: 40 bytes for 17 FLOPs, AI = 0.425. Attainable 0.425 × 320 = 136 GFLOP/s.
  3. With perfect reuse each pixel reads 1 float and writes 1: 8 bytes, AI = 2.125, attainable 680 GFLOP/s.
  4. Memory bound either way. Tiling gives up to 5 times, but the ceiling is still 680 of 8141 GFLOP/s
Problem B. Launch one thread per element for N = 1,000,000 with 256 threads per block on that T4 (1024 threads and 16 blocks per SM). Give the blocks, idle threads, total warps and waves.
  1. Blocks = ⌈1,000,000 ÷ 256⌉ = 3907. Threads launched = 1,000,192, so 192 are idle.
  2. Warps = 3907 × 8 = 31,256.
  3. Blocks per SM: threads allow 1024 ÷ 256 = 4, the block cap allows 16, so 4. One wave holds 40 × 4 = 160 blocks.
  4. Waves = ⌈3907 ÷ 160⌉ = 25, the last one 42 percent full
Problem C. Sum 220 floats with 512 threads per block, a tree in shared memory and one atomic per block. Count blocks, levels, barriers per block and all additions.
  1. Blocks = 220 ÷ 512 = 2048. Levels = log₂ 512 = 9. Barriers = 1 after loading + 9 = 10.
  2. Additions inside blocks = 2048 × 511 = 1,046,528. Atomics = 2048.
  3. Together 1,048,576 = 220, one addition per input value counting the add onto the zero total.
  4. Same work as a serial sum, in 9 levels instead of 1,048,575 steps
Problem D. You sum 4096 ones in FP16. What do you get, and why does a tensor core avoid it?
  1. FP16 has 10 mantissa bits, so the gap between numbers at 2048 is 211 − 10 = 2.
  2. 2048 + 1 = 2049 lies halfway, and round to nearest even gives 2048. Every later add is lost.
  3. Tensor cores take FP16 inputs but add in FP32, where the gap at 2048 is far below 1.
  4. FP16 sum = 2048, FP32 accumulator = 4096
Problem E. Copies take 20 percent of a job and the kernel takes 80 percent. You make the kernel 50 times faster. What is the overall speedup, and what should you do next?
  1. Speedup of a part: 1 ÷ ((1 − p) + p ÷ s) with p = 0.8 and s = 50.
  2. 1 ÷ (0.2 + 0.016) = 1 ÷ 0.216 = 4.63.
  3. The copies are now 0.2 ÷ 0.216 = 93 percent of the time.
  4. 4.63 times. Next: keep data on the GPU or overlap the copies with streams
Lesson 8 of 8

From intuition to research

One idea, four depths

A GPU is fast when many small jobs run at once and the data is close by. Almost every formula here counts one of those two things.

For any kernel, write down threads, warps, bytes, FLOPs and the limiting resource. Then pick the formula.

Combine models: roofline for the ceiling, occupancy and Little's law for latency hiding, waves for the tail, Amdahl for the copies.

Research on GPU kernels checks these models against Nsight measurements and then attacks the gap, as in the fast matmul ladder of chapter 8.3.
