From one label to a label per pixel

Each vision task adds a question. Classification asks what the image shows. Detection asks what and roughly where. Semantic segmentation asks what is at every single pixel. The lecturer put it as semantic understanding plus spatial precision: features are extracted, every pixel gets a score per class, and the scores become the final mask.

TaskQuestionOutputSize
ClassificationWhat is this image?One label1 label (C scores)
DetectionWhat, and roughly where?Class, box and score per objectN × (4 + 1 + C)
Semantic segmentationWhat, and exactly where?A class for every pixel (a mask)H × W labels (H × W × C scores)
Semantic segmentationExam favourite
I ∈ ℝH × W × 3 → Y ∈ {1, …, C}H × W
One classification repeated at every pixel. Tasks that predict something at every pixel are called dense prediction.

Why a box is not enough

  • A self driving car needs the exact drivable road pixels. The road has no meaningful box at all, it is a region.
  • For a tumour, area, growth and the exact outline drive treatment. A rectangle overestimates the area.
  • Even a perfect ellipse fills only πab ÷ (2a · 2b) = π ÷ 4 ≈ 78.5% of its box. A 4 pixel lane marking running diagonally across a 100 × 100 box fills about 566 pixels, 5.7% of it.

A student asked: if segmentation is better, why not use it everywhere? The lecturer gave three reasons. It is heavy to compute (a 512 × 512 image needs 262,144 predictions instead of 1). Every pixel needs a label, so annotation is slow (the Cityscapes authors report more than 1.5 hours per finely labelled image). And if classification answers the question, use classification.

Panoptic quality
PQ = Σmatched IoU|TP| + ½|FP| + ½|FN| = SQ × RQ
Segmentation quality (mean IoU of matched segments) times recognition quality (an F1 style count). Three matches with IoU 0.8, 0.7, 0.9, one FP and one FN: SQ = 0.80, RQ = 3 ÷ 4 = 0.75, PQ = 0.60.
Lesson 2 of 4

What the network outputs

The input stays a 256 × 256 × 3 image. Before the final decision the output is 256 × 256 × C. The lecturer: the channels become classes. Channel k is a score map for class k, high where the network believes class k is present. The final mask is 256 × 256, one class ID per pixel.

SoftMax at each pixelExam favourite
P(c | x, y) = ezc(x, y)Σk ezk(x, y)
Positive, and the C values add to 1. Road competes with the other four classes, car with the other four, and so on.
Label
Y(x, y) = argmaxc P(c | x, y)
Before argmax: H × W × C. After argmax: H × W.
From raw scores to a label
  1. Classes road, car, person, building, sky. Logits z = [0.00, −0.51, −0.91, 0.70, 2.78].
  2. Exponentials: 1.000, 0.600, 0.403, 2.014, 16.119. Their sum is 20.136.
  3. Divide: p = 0.050, 0.030, 0.020, 0.100, 0.801. They add to 1.
  4. The largest is sky, so this pixel becomes sky.
Going deeper: Why argmax of SoftMax equals argmax of the logits

Every class shares the same positive denominator, and the exponential is strictly increasing. If za > zb then eza > ezb and pa > pb. The order is preserved, so inference can skip SoftMax. It is still needed for the loss and for confidence values.

Why SoftMax and not sigmoid? A student answered well: sigmoids do not add up to one. Two sigmoids can give 0.9 and 0.5 for the same pixel. Use SoftMax when each pixel has exactly one class. Use one sigmoid per class when a pixel may carry several labels at once, such as a vessel inside a lesion.

The ground truth

A mask is a matrix of class IDs. Colours are only for display. The slide used a 5 × 5 mask with 0 sky, 1 building, 2 road, 3 car and 4 person.

0000001110003332222422224
Read a mask like the network does
  1. Count each ID: sky 7, building 3, road 10, car 3, person 2. Total 25.
  2. Shares: road 40%, sky 28%, building 12%, car 12%, person 8%. Already imbalanced.
  3. For the loss, each pixel becomes a one hot vector. A person pixel becomes [0, 0, 0, 0, 1], so the mask becomes a 5 × 5 × 5 tensor.
  4. A pixel labelled 255 would be an ignore label and get no loss at all.
Remember
Dense labels are expensive. Someone must trace every boundary. When masks are scarce, people use weak labels (tags, boxes, points or scribbles), a few masks plus many unlabelled images, self supervised pre-training, or a click based tool such as SAM to speed up annotation.
Lesson 3 of 4

Why a classifier loses the WHERE

A classification network shrinks 224 × 224 × 3 down to 7 × 7 × 2048, then pools to one vector and one label. That is on purpose: it should say dog wherever the dog is. Segmentation needs the opposite, a label map that moves with the object.

Classification wantsSegmentation needs
Invariance: location does not matterPrecision: every boundary matters
A summary of the whole imageSmall objects must not disappear
One vector, one labelThe output must keep the spatial grid
Going deeper: Convolution is equivariant, global pooling is invariant

Let y[n] = Σk w[k] x[n − k]. Shift the input by a: x′[n] = x[n − a]. Then y′[n] = Σk w[k] x[n − a − k] = y[n − a]. Shifting then convolving equals convolving then shifting. That is equivariance.

Global average pooling averages over all positions, so the shift disappears: the output is invariant. Segmentation wants the equivariant part and must not throw it away with pooling and fully connected layers. Strided layers are only roughly equivariant, because a shift that is not a multiple of the stride changes the output.

Receptive field

The students defined it well: how much of the original image one neuron can see. The kernel stays 3 × 3, but its coverage in input pixels grows with depth and stride.

Receptive field recurrenceExam favourite
rl = rl−1 + (k − 1) · dl · jl−1,   jl = jl−1 · sl
Start with r = 1 and j = 1. k is the kernel size, d the dilation, s the stride, j the jump between neighbouring cells in input pixels.
Four 3 × 3 layers
  1. Plain layers each add 2: r = 3, 5, 7, 9.
  2. Give layers 2 and 4 stride 2: r = 3, 5 (j becomes 2), 9, 13 (j becomes 4).
  3. Downsampling bought a field of 13 instead of 9, but the map is now 4 times smaller in each direction. Resolution down, receptive field up.

The downsampling trade-off

Downsampling improvesDownsampling loses
Larger receptive fieldFine boundaries
More abstract, semantic featuresThin structures such as lane markings and blood vessels
Context, and less computationSmall objects

A structure of 16 × 8 pixels becomes 4 × 2 cells after ×4, 2 × 1 after ×8, and one cell or less after ×16. A 4 pixel lane marking at stride 16 covers a quarter of a cell and is outvoted by background.

Going deeper: Why the decoder cannot invent what the encoder dropped

Let X be the image, Z the bottleneck and Ŷ the decoder output, a function of Z only. The data processing inequality says I(X, Ŷ) ≤ I(X, Z): the output cannot carry more information about the image than the bottleneck kept. The fix is to give the decoder other paths to the input, which is what skip connections do in the next chapter.

Depth

The lecturer's example: photograph the classroom and a depth map shows the front row close and each later row further away. Students listed uses: driving, robotic surgery, crop growth tracking, terrain from the air, pits on turbine blades and warehouse robots. Depth comes from two cameras (stereo), from LiDAR or time of flight, or from one image with a learned model.

Optical flow

One pixel's flow
  1. A bright point is at (10, 10) in frame t and at (13, 14) in frame t + 1.
  2. Flow (u, v) = (3, 4). Magnitude √(9 + 16) = 5 pixels per frame.
  3. The direction is arctan(4 ÷ 3) ≈ 53° below the horizontal, since image y grows downwards. The whole flow field is H × W × 2.
Remember
Same body, different head. An FCN, U-Net or DeepLab body is reused across the family. Change the last layer from C class channels to 1 channel (depth), 2 (flow) or K (heat maps) and change the loss to match.
One idea, four depths

Segmentation colours in every pixel of a photo with what it shows: road, car, person or sky. A box only says roughly where something is. A mask gives its exact shape, which matters for a road, a tumour or a weed.

The network outputs H × W × C scores. SoftMax at each pixel gives probabilities, and argmax picks one class. Semantic masks cannot count touching objects, instance masks can, panoptic does both. Downsampling helps recognition but erases thin and small structures.

Convolutions are translation equivariant and global pooling is invariant, so segmentation keeps the grid. The receptive field grows as r += (k − 1) d j. Information lost in the encoder is gone for good by the data processing inequality, so architectures must add paths around the bottleneck. PQ = SQ × RQ scores panoptic output.

Label efficient segmentation (weak, semi and self supervised learning, and foundation model assisted labelling) is a major research theme. Strided networks are not truly shift equivariant, and anti-aliased downsampling (blur before subsampling) restores much of it. Panoptic methods now use one mask classification model for semantic, instance and panoptic output.
