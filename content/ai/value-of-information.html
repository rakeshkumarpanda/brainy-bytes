What is it worth to know first?

You must decide whether to do an MBA, which costs 10, before you know how the job market will look when you graduate. Nature makes the market good with 0.25, medium with 0.5 and poor with 0.25.

Market (probability)With MBA, grossWith MBA, net of 10Without MBA
Good (0.25)322212
Medium (0.5)1668
Poor (0.25)1224
Case A: decide before the market is known
  1. E[MBA] = 0.25 × 22 + 0.5 × 6 + 0.25 × 2 = 5.5 + 3 + 0.5 = 9.
  2. E[no MBA] = 0.25 × 12 + 0.5 × 8 + 0.25 × 4 = 3 + 4 + 1 = 8.
  3. Get the MBA, expected payoff 9
Case B: an oracle tells you the market first
  1. Good: max(22, 12) = 22, get the MBA.
  2. Medium: max(6, 8) = 8, skip it. Poor: max(2, 4) = 4, skip it.
  3. E[with information] = 0.25 × 22 + 0.5 × 8 + 0.25 × 4 = 10.5
Value of perfect informationExam favourite
VOI = E[ maxa u(a, s) ] − maxa E[ u(a, s) ]
Best with the oracle (choose per state, then average) minus best without (average per action, then choose).
MBA exampleExam favourite
VOI = 10.5 − 9 = 1.5
Pay an oracle anything below 1.5. Above 1.5 you are better off deciding now.
Common mistake
A student in class computed 0.25 × 22 + 0.5 × 8 + 0.25 × 4 as the no information value. That mixes actions across states, which you can only do once you know the state. Without information you must use one action in every state.
Lesson 2 of 4

Information is never worth less than zero

With the oracle you could always ignore what it says and take the action you would have taken anyway. So the best plan with information is at least as good as the best plan without it.

Proof that VOI ≥ 0
  1. Let a* be the best action without information, so the no information value is E[u(a*, s)].
  2. For every state s, maxa u(a, s) ≥ u(a*, s), since a* is one of the options.
  3. Take the expectation of both sides over s.
  4. E[maxa u(a, s)] ≥ E[u(a*, s)], so VOI ≥ 0
  5. Equality holds exactly when one action is best in every state. The oracle then never changes your choice.
Practice problem C: book an outdoor or indoor venue before knowing the weather
  1. Sunny 0.5, rainy 0.5. Outdoor pays 50 or −20. Indoor pays 30 or 25.
  2. No forecast: E[outdoor] = 15 and E[indoor] = 27.5, so book indoor, value 27.5.
  3. Perfect forecast: sunny pick outdoor 50, rainy pick indoor 25, so 0.5 × 50 + 0.5 × 25 = 37.5.
  4. VOI = 37.5 − 27.5 = 10
Real world scenario: exploratory drilling

An oil company can buy a lease at price X. From geology it already has probabilities for how oily the site is, which give an expected value Y without any survey. A survey costs money but reveals more. If X is far below Y the deal is good enough already and the survey adds little. If the survey is cheap compared with what it might change, survey first. The probabilities are known before drilling, exactly like the market probabilities in the MBA example. This is also one reason firms negotiate options on two or three sites instead of surveying one in depth.

Lesson 3 of 4

Imperfect information and Bayes

Real forecasts are not oracles. Suppose a forecast is right with probability q. When it says "sunny", update your belief with Bayes' rule, choose the best action for the updated belief, and average over what the forecast might say.

Bayes updateExam favourite
P(s | signal) = P(signal | s) P(s)P(signal)
The forecast shifts the probabilities, not the payoffs.
Value of imperfect information
VOI = ∑signals P(signal) maxa E[u(a, s) | signal] − maxa E[u(a, s)]
Between 0 and the value of perfect information.
Venue problem with a forecast that is right 90% of the time
  1. Forecast says sunny with probability 0.5. Then P(sunny | says sunny) = 0.45/0.5 = 0.9.
  2. Outdoor: 0.9 × 50 + 0.1 × (−20) = 43. Indoor: 0.9 × 30 + 0.1 × 25 = 29.5. Choose outdoor, 43.
  3. Forecast says rainy: P(sunny) = 0.1. Outdoor 5 − 18 = −13, indoor 3 + 22.5 = 25.5. Choose indoor, 25.5.
  4. Expected value with the forecast: 0.5 × 43 + 0.5 × 25.5 = 34.25.
  5. VOI = 34.25 − 27.5 = 6.75, less than 10 for a perfect forecast
Going deeper: A forecast that is right half the time is worth nothing

With q = 0.5 the posterior after either signal equals the prior, 0.5 and 0.5. The best action after each signal is the same as without the forecast, so the value is exactly 0. Information has value only if it can change what you do. Below q = 0.5 the forecast is still useful: reverse what it says.

One idea, four depths

Finding out before you decide can only help, because you can always ignore what you learn. It is worth paying for only when it might change your choice.

VOI = E[best per state] − best E[per action]. MBA: 10.5 − 9 = 1.5. Venue: 37.5 − 27.5 = 10. VOI ≥ 0, and it is 0 when one action wins in every state.

For imperfect signals, compute posteriors by Bayes and the value of sample information. Blackwell's theorem (1953) orders information structures: one experiment is more informative than another exactly when every decision maker values it at least as much.

In active learning and Bayesian optimization, the next query is chosen to maximize expected information or knowledge gradient, a direct use of value of information. Exploration bonuses in reinforcement learning, such as information directed sampling, trade the value of information against the immediate regret.

Lesson 4 of 4

From intuition to research

Information is worth paying for only if it can change what you do. This lesson computes that worth and shows why it is never negative.

Real world scenario: a weather forecast before a picnic

An outdoor picnic is worth 100 if sunny and 0 if it rains. An indoor party is worth 60 whatever the weather. Rain has probability 0.3. How much should you pay for a perfect forecast?

Work it out: the value of perfect information
  1. Without a forecast: outdoors gives 0.7 × 100 = 70, indoors 60. Choose outdoors, 70.
  2. With a forecast: sunny (0.7) choose outdoors 100, rain (0.3) choose indoors 60.
  3. Expected value with the forecast: 0.7 × 100 + 0.3 × 60 = 88.
  4. Value of perfect information = 88 − 70 = 18
Going deeper: the analysis

Information is never worth less than zero: with information you could always make the same choice as without it, so E[maxa V(a | signal)] ≥ maxa E[V(a | signal)]. The gap is zero exactly when the best action is the same for every signal. Imperfect information is valued the same way, with posterior probabilities from Bayes’ rule.

Going deeper: the research view

Active learning and Bayesian optimisation pick the next experiment or label by its expected value of information or information gain. AI assistants that ask a clarifying question before acting use the same calculation: ask only if the answer could change the action enough to repay the cost of asking.

Common mistake
If every forecast would lead to the same choice, the forecast is worth 0 to you, however accurate it is.
