Lesson 1 of 10

The idea: destroy data with noise, then learn to undo it

Source: Sohl-Dickstein and colleagues (2015), and Ho, Jain and Abbeel, Denoising diffusion probabilistic models (2020).

What it is
A diffusion model has two processes. The forward process is fixed and needs no learning: over T steps (1000 in DDPM) it adds a little Gaussian noise to the data each time, until only pure noise is left. The reverse process is learned: a network learns to remove a little noise at a time, so that starting from pure noise and running it backwards produces brand new data.
Why we need it
Turning noise into an image in one step is very hard, which is why GANs are unstable. Removing a small amount of noise is an easy, well defined task, and many easy steps add up to one hard one. Diffusion models train with a simple regression loss, do not collapse onto a few modes, and now power most image, video and audio generators.
How it works
Pick a noise schedule β1 to βT. Thanks to a closed form you can jump straight to any step t: xt = √{AB}t x0 + √(1 − {AB}t) ε. Train a network to look at xt and t and predict ε. To generate, start from noise and remove the predicted noise step by step.

Real world scenario: Restoring an old photo, many times over

Imagine a photo that someone sprinkles with a little dust every second for a whole day, until it is pure grey speckle. A restorer who has watched thousands of photos get dusty learns one small skill: given a dusty photo and how long it has been sprinkled, guess the dust that was added last and brush it off. Applied a thousand times to pure speckle, that one small skill paints a brand new photo that never existed.

Lesson 2 of 10

Training: predict the noise

Source: Ho, Jain and Abbeel (2020), Algorithm 1 and section 3.4 (the simplified loss).

What it is
The network εθ(xt, t) gets a noisy example and the step number and predicts the noise ε that was mixed in. The loss is the mean squared error between the true and the predicted noise.
Why we need it
The full training objective is a variational bound, like the VAE’s ELBO. Ho and colleagues showed that it simplifies, with one change of weights, into plain noise prediction, which trains more stably and gives better samples. Predicting noise also means the target always has the same scale, whatever the step.
How it works
Repeat: take a real example x0, pick a random step t from 1 to T, draw noise ε, build xt with the closed form, and take a gradient step on ||ε − εθ(xt, t)||². No sampling chain is run during training, so it is as cheap as training a regression network.
Download this program (.py)Save it, open it in any editor and run it with python3 ddpm_1d.py.
Remember
The model is only a linear layer on smooth features of x and t, fitted in one least squares solve, but it is trained with exactly the DDPM loss. Its loss is 0.253, far below the 1.000 of always guessing zero. Sampled with 1000 DDPM steps it makes new numbers whose bumps have mean −1.99 and 2.01 and spread 0.29, against −2.00, 2.01 and 0.30 in the real data.
Training, one step at a time (Algorithm 1)
  1. Take a batch of real examples x0.
  2. For each, draw a step t uniformly from 1 to T, and noise ε ~ N(0, I).
  3. xt = √āt x0 + √(1 − āt) ε
  4. Ask the network for its guess of the noise, εθ(xt, t).
  5. L = || ε − εθ(xt, t) ||², then one gradient step
  6. Repeat for hundreds of thousands of steps.
Simple DDPM lossExam favourite
Lsimple = Et, x0, ε || ε − εθ(xt, t) ||²
A regression loss on the noise.
Noise, data and score are the same thingExam favourite
score ∇ ln pt(xt) ≈ −εθ / √(1 − āt)
Predicting noise is learning the direction towards more likely data.
Going deeper: Where the simple loss comes from

Like a VAE, the model maximises a lower bound on ln p(x0). Because every step is Gaussian, the bound becomes a sum of KL divergences between Gaussians, one per step, between the true reverse step q(xt−1 | xt, x0), which has a closed form, and the model’s step. Writing the model’s mean in terms of predicted noise turns each KL term into a weighted squared error on ε. Dropping the weights gives Lsimple, which puts more effort on the noisier, harder steps. Later work (EDM, v prediction, min SNR weighting) chooses the weights on purpose instead of dropping them.

Lesson 3 of 10

Sampling: DDPM and the faster DDIM

Source: Ho, Jain and Abbeel (2020), Algorithm 2, and Song, Meng and Ermon, Denoising diffusion implicit models (2021).

What it is
DDPM sampling starts from noise xT and, for t = T down to 1, removes the predicted noise and adds a smaller amount of fresh noise. DDIM uses the same trained network but takes big deterministic jumps: predict the clean example, then move to a much earlier step without fresh noise.
Why we need it
DDPM needs one network call per step, 1000 for one image, which is slow. DDIM gives nearly the same quality in 25 to 50 calls, and because it is deterministic the same starting noise always gives the same image, which makes editing and interpolation possible.
How it works
Choose the sampler and the number of steps when you generate, not when you train. Fewer steps are faster but, below a point, the samples blur towards the average of the data.

In the exam

Conditioning, guidance and text to image

Source: Dhariwal and Nichol (2021), Ho and Salimans, Classifier free diffusion guidance (2022), and Rombach and colleagues, High resolution image synthesis with latent diffusion models (2022).

What it is
A conditional diffusion model also gets a condition c, such as a class or a text prompt, and predicts εθ(xt, t, c). Classifier free guidance trains one network both with and without the condition (the condition is dropped about 10 percent of the time) and at sampling time pushes away from the unconditional prediction.
Why we need it
Plain conditioning follows the prompt only loosely. Guidance makes samples match the condition much more closely, at the cost of variety. It is the guidance scale slider in every text to image tool.
How it works
Compute both predictions at each step and mix them: (1 + w) εcond − w εuncond. Small w keeps variety, large w gives sharp, typical, less varied samples. Each step now costs two network calls.

Going deeper: How a text to image system fits together

A latent diffusion system such as Stable Diffusion has three parts. A VAE (Deep learning 3.1) compresses each image into a small latent and decodes latents back to pixels. A text encoder (CLIP or T5) turns the prompt into vectors. A denoiser, first a U-Net with cross attention to the text (Deep learning 2.1 and 2.3) and now often a transformer on latent patches (DiT), runs the diffusion process in latent space. Generation is: encode the prompt, start from latent noise, run 20 to 50 guided DDIM like steps, decode the latent into an image. Video and audio models use the same recipe on other latents.

Noise schedules and the denoiser network

Source: Nichol and Dhariwal, Improved denoising diffusion probabilistic models (2021), and Karras and colleagues, Elucidating the design space of diffusion based generative models (2022).

What it is
The noise schedule decides how fast {AB}t falls from 1 to 0. The linear schedule of DDPM raises βt evenly from 0.0001 to 0.02. The cosine schedule makes {AB}t follow a cosine curve.
Why we need it
With the linear schedule the data is almost destroyed halfway through, so many late steps only shuffle noise and teach the network little. The cosine schedule spreads the destruction more evenly, which helps small images most.
How it works
Compare the schedules by when the signal to noise ratio reaches 1 and by how many steps are almost pure noise. The denoiser is usually a U-Net (Deep learning 2.1) that also receives t through a sinusoidal embedding, like the positional encoding of transformers (Deep learning 2.3).

Remember

Bottom line: Diffusion is the default for images, video and audio when quality and variety matter most. GANs remain where one fast pass is essential, and VAEs supply the latent space that diffusion runs in.

Going deeper: Score models, SDEs and flow matching

Song and Ermon trained networks to estimate the score ∇ ln p(x) of the data at many noise levels and sampled with Langevin dynamics. Song and colleagues (2021) showed that DDPM and score models are two discretisations of one stochastic differential equation, and that every such SDE has a deterministic probability flow ODE with the same distributions at every time, which is what DDIM follows. Flow matching and rectified flow (2023) learn the velocity of a straight path from noise to data directly. They are now used in many leading image models because the straighter paths need fewer sampling steps. Distillation methods such as consistency models compress sampling to one to four steps.

The maths, all in one place

Forward stepExam favourite
q(xt | xt−1) = N(√αt xt−1, βt I),   αt = 1 − βt
Fixed, no parameters.
Closed formExam favourite
q(xt | x0) = N(√āt x0, (1 − āt) I),   āt = ∏s≤t αs
Jump straight to any step.
True reverse step given x0Exam favourite
q(xt−1 | xt, x0) = N(μ̃t, β̃t I),   β̃t = (1 − āt−1) / (1 − āt) · βt
The target each model step tries to match.
Model mean from predicted noiseExam favourite
μθ = (xt − βt / √(1 − āt) · εθ) / √αt
Used in the DDPM step.
Tweedie formula
E[x0 | xt] = (xt + (1 − āt) ∇ ln pt(xt)) / √āt
Denoising and the score are linked exactly.
Worked example: why the closed form holds
  1. Write two forward steps with independent noises ε1 and ε2.
  2. x1 = √α1 x0 + √(1 − α1) ε1,   x2 = √α2 x1 + √(1 − α2) ε2
  3. Substitute x1 into x2.
  4. x2 = √(α1α2) x0 + √α2 √(1 − α1) ε1 + √(1 − α2) ε2
  5. A sum of independent Gaussians is Gaussian, and the variances add.
  6. α2(1 − α1) + (1 − α2) = 1 − α1α2
  7. So x2 = √ā2 x0 + √(1 − ā2) ε with ā2 = α1α2. Induction gives every t.

Chapter summary

  1. The forward process adds Gaussian noise over T steps. Any step in one jump: xt = √āt x0 + √(1 − āt) ε.
  2. Training is regression: predict the added noise with the loss ||ε − εθ(xt, t)||². Predicting noise is the same as learning the score.
  3. DDPM samples with 1000 small noisy steps. DDIM jumps deterministically and matched it with 50 steps, but 3 steps blurred the samples.
  4. Classifier free guidance (1 + w) εcond − w εuncond follows the condition more closely and narrows the variety (spread 0.27 to 0.13).
  5. Latent diffusion runs in a VAE latent 48 times smaller than the pixels, with a text encoder and a U-Net or DiT. The cosine schedule wastes fewer steps on pure noise.
