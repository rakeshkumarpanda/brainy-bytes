Lesson 1 of 9

Attention: every word looks at every other word

Source: Bahdanau, Cho and Bengio (ICLR 2015), Vaswani and colleagues (NeurIPS 2017) section 3.2, and the Stanford CS224n notes on attention.

What it is
Attention lets each position build its new representation from all positions at once. Every token gets a query (what it is looking for), a key (what it offers) and a value (what it passes on), each a learned linear map of its embedding. A token scores every key with a dot product, turns the scores into weights with softmax, and outputs the weighted sum of the values.
Why we need it
An RNN has to carry information step by step, so distant words are hard to connect and training cannot run in parallel over time. With attention, any two positions are one step apart, and all positions are computed at once on a GPU. This is what made very large language models possible.
How it works
Stack queries, keys and values into matrices Q, K and V and compute softmax(QKT/√dk)V in one go. Divide by √dk so scores do not grow with the dimension. For text generation, add a causal mask so each position sees only earlier ones.

In the exam
With 4 hand picked tokens, the first query puts weight 0.539 on the key that points the same way and 0.065 on the one that points away. A query of all zeros gives equal weights of 0.25, so it simply averages the values. With the causal mask, the first token can only attend to itself (weight 1). For random vectors, the spread of q · k grows from 2.0 at d = 4 to 22.6 at d = 512, and dividing by √d brings it back to 1.

Multi head attention and the transformer block

Source: Vaswani and colleagues (NeurIPS 2017) sections 3.1 to 3.5, Ba, Kiros and Hinton (2016, layer normalisation), and Xiong and colleagues, On layer normalization in the transformer architecture (ICML 2020).

What it is
Multi head attention runs several attention operations in parallel on smaller slices of the vectors, then joins and mixes the results. A transformer block is multi head attention followed by a position wise feed forward network, each wrapped in a residual connection and layer normalisation. Because attention ignores order, a positional encoding is added to the token embeddings.
Why we need it
Different heads learn different relations, for example one head links pronouns to nouns and another links verbs to objects. The feed forward network then processes each position on its own. Residuals and layer norm keep deep stacks trainable, just as in ResNets. Without positions, "dog bites man" and "man bites dog" would look the same.
How it works
Use dmodel = heads × dhead (for example 768 = 12 × 64) and a feed forward size of 4 dmodel. Modern models put layer norm before each sub layer (pre norm) for stability, and often use learned or rotary positional encodings instead of fixed sinusoids.

In the exam
A block maps 6 vectors of size 16 to 6 vectors of size 16, and each head’s attention rows sum to 1. Without positional encodings, reversing the input tokens only reverses the outputs, which shows that attention alone cannot see order. One block with dmodel = 768 has about 7.1 million weights, so BERT base’s 12 blocks hold 85 million. GPT 3’s 96 blocks of size 12,288 hold about 174 billion.

Why attention beats recurrence on long distances

Source: Vaswani and colleagues (2017) section 4 and table 1, Bahdanau and colleagues (2015), and the memory task from chapter 2.2.

What it is
In an RNN, information from step 1 must survive every update until step T, a path of length T. In self attention, the last position reads step 1 directly, a path of length 1. Attention also computes all positions in parallel, while an RNN must go one step at a time.
Why we need it
Short paths mean gradients do not vanish with distance, so long range patterns are easy to learn. Parallel computation means far more data can be processed in the same time, and scale has turned out to be the main driver of quality.
How it works
The price is that attention compares every pair of positions, so its cost grows with T². Long context models use tricks such as FlashAttention (exact but memory efficient), sliding windows, sparse patterns or state space layers to manage this.

BERT, GPT and how text is generated

Source: Devlin and colleagues (NAACL 2019), Radford and colleagues (2018, 2019), Brown and colleagues (NeurIPS 2020), Ouyang and colleagues, InstructGPT (NeurIPS 2022), and Holtzman and colleagues (ICLR 2020).

What it is
BERT is a stack of encoder blocks pretrained to fill in masked words, so it sees context on both sides and is used for understanding tasks. GPT is a stack of decoder blocks with a causal mask, pretrained to predict the next token, so it can generate text. A language model outputs a probability for every token in its vocabulary, and a decoding rule picks the next one.
Why we need it
Pretraining on huge amounts of text teaches grammar, facts and reasoning patterns once, and then a model can be adapted to many tasks with little labelled data, through fine tuning or just a prompt. How you decode changes the output a lot: always taking the top token gives dull, repetitive text, while sampling adds variety.
How it works
Fine tune BERT style models for classification and extraction. Use GPT style models for generation and chat, adapted with instruction tuning and RLHF or DPO (the Machine Learning subject, Reinforcement chapter 3.3). Control sampling with temperature, top k and top p, and measure language models with perplexity.

In the exam
For "The cat sat on the", temperature 0.5 gives "mat" a probability of 0.867, temperature 2 lowers it to 0.456 and spreads the rest. Top k = 2 keeps mat and sofa. Top p = 0.9 keeps mat, sofa and roof, which hold 0.984 of the probability. True next words with probabilities 0.60, 0.25, 0.10 and 0.40 give a perplexity of 3.59. Going from 2,048 to 8,192 tokens multiplies the attention scores per head by 16.

Worked example: attention for one query by hand
  1. Query q = (1, 0). Keys k1 = (1, 0) and k2 = (0, 1). Values v1 = (1, 0) and v2 = (0, 1). dk = 2.
  2. scores = (1, 0) / √2 = (0.7071, 0)
  3. Softmax.
  4. w1 = e0.7071 / (e0.7071 + 1) = 2.028 / 3.028 = 0.6698,   w2 = 0.3302
  5. Weighted sum of values.
  6. output = 0.6698 (1, 0) + 0.3302 (0, 1) = (0.6698, 0.3302)
  7. The query matched the first key better, so the output leans towards the first value, but softmax still gives the second a share.
Going deeper: What attention heads learn, and scaling

Studies of trained transformers find heads that track syntax, copy earlier tokens or attend to the previous token. Olsson and colleagues (2022) describe induction heads, pairs of heads that find an earlier occurrence of the current token and copy what followed it, which appear suddenly during training and support in context learning. Scaling laws (Kaplan and colleagues 2020, Hoffmann and colleagues 2022) show test loss falling as a power law in parameters, data and compute, with the Chinchilla result that parameters and training tokens should grow together, about 20 tokens per parameter. Efficient variants change the attention itself: FlashAttention reorders the computation to avoid storing the T × T matrix, multi query and grouped query attention share keys and values across heads to shrink the KV cache, and rotary position embeddings (RoPE, Su and colleagues 2021) encode relative position directly in the dot product.

Chapter summary

  1. Attention: softmax(QKT/√dk)V. Each token scores every key, softmax gives weights, and the output blends the values.
  2. Multi head attention runs several attentions in parallel. A transformer block adds a feed forward network, residuals and layer norm. Positional encodings supply word order.
  3. Attention connects any two positions in one step and runs in parallel, so it learns long range patterns that RNNs miss, at a cost of O(T²).
  4. BERT (encoder, masked tokens) understands, GPT (decoder, causal mask, next token) generates. Decoding uses temperature, top k and top p, and quality is measured with perplexity.
  5. One block has about 12d² weights. Large models stack dozens of blocks and need a KV cache when generating.
