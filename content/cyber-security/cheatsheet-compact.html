1.1What cybersecurity is: goals, threats and layers

  • Cybersecurity Protects systems and data from people who want to steal, change or stop them. People, process and technology together.
  • CIA triad Confidentiality (right people read), integrity (no unnoticed change), availability (works when needed). AAA: authentication, authorisation, accounting.
  • Words Asset, threat, vulnerability (weakness), exploit (method), risk = likelihood × impact, control, attack surface, zero day, CVE.
  • Defence in depth Independent layers: P(breach) = product of miss chances. Many tries: P(at least one) = 1 − (1 − p)n.
  • Principles Least privilege, fail safe defaults, complete mediation, economy of mechanism, open design (Kerckhoffs), separation of privilege, zero trust.
  • Money Expected loss = probability × cost. Gordon and Loeb: best spend under about 37% (1/e) of expected loss.
  • AAAAuthentication (who are you), Authorisation (what may you do), Accounting (what did you do)
  • Risk in one linerisk = likelihood × impact
  • Chance that an attack passes every layerP(breach) = m1 × m2 × ... × mk
  • Chance that at least one of many attempts succeedsP(at least one) = 1 − (1 − p)n
  • Expected lossexpected loss = probability × cost
  • Gordon and Loeb boundbest spend ≤ (1 / e) × expected loss ≈ 0.368 × expected loss
  • Exam Goals can pull against each other. Locking a file with a strong password raises confidentiality but can hurt availability if the password is lost. Good security balances all three for each asset.
  • Trap A vulnerability is the weakness, an exploit is the method that uses it, and a threat is who might use it. Students often swap these three in exams.
  • Exam The attacker needs to win once. The defender needs to win every time. Layers change this: now the attacker must win at every layer, every time.
  • Exam Open design is also called Kerckhoffs’s principle (1883): a cipher should stay safe even if the enemy knows everything about it except the key. Hiding the method is called security through obscurity, and it is not enough on its own.
  • Trap Exam details (number of questions, time, passing score, price) change every few years. Always check the official page of CompTIA, EC-Council, ISC2 or OffSec before you book. The revision chapter at the end of this subject lists them as checked when it was written.
