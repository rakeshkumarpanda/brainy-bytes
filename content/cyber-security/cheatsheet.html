1.1

What cybersecurity is: goals, threats and layers

Open chapter

the CIA triad, the words of security, attackers and real incidents, defence in depth with its maths, design principles, zero trust and careers.

Key ideas

  • Cybersecurity Protects systems and data from people who want to steal, change or stop them. People, process and technology together.
  • CIA triad Confidentiality (right people read), integrity (no unnoticed change), availability (works when needed). AAA: authentication, authorisation, accounting.
  • Words Asset, threat, vulnerability (weakness), exploit (method), risk = likelihood × impact, control, attack surface, zero day, CVE.
  • Defence in depth Independent layers: P(breach) = product of miss chances. Many tries: P(at least one) = 1 − (1 − p)n.
  • Principles Least privilege, fail safe defaults, complete mediation, economy of mechanism, open design (Kerckhoffs), separation of privilege, zero trust.
  • Money Expected loss = probability × cost. Gordon and Loeb: best spend under about 37% (1/e) of expected loss.

Formulas

Three more words that go with CIA
Authenticity: it really comes from who it says. Non repudiation: the sender cannot later deny sending it. Accountability: every action can be traced to a person.
Some books add these to make five goals. The Parkerian hexad (Donn Parker, 1998) uses six: confidentiality, possession, integrity, authenticity, availability and utility.
Lesson: The three goals: confidentiality, integrity and availability
AAAExam favourite
Authentication (who are you), Authorisation (what may you do), Accounting (what did you do)
The three steps every login system follows. Exam questions often mix them up.
Lesson: The three goals: confidentiality, integrity and availability
Risk in one lineExam favourite
risk = likelihood × impact
Likelihood is how often it is expected to happen. Impact is how much damage one event does. Chapter 1.2 turns this into money with SLE, ARO and ALE.

No threat or no weakness, no risk
risk exists only when a threat can use a vulnerability against an asset
A locked window with no burglars around, or burglars with no way in, gives little risk.
Lesson: The words every security person uses
Chance that an attack passes every layerExam favourite
P(breach) = m1 × m2 × ... × mk
mi is the chance that layer i misses. Only true when the layers fail independently.
Lesson: Defence in depth: many layers, not one wall
Chance that at least one of many attempts succeedsExam favourite
P(at least one) = 1 − (1 − p)n
p is the chance one attempt works, n is the number of attempts. Attackers only need to win once.
Lesson: Defence in depth: many layers, not one wall
Expected lossExam favourite
expected loss = probability × cost
The average loss per year if the same risk is faced many years. Used to compare risks of very different kinds.
Lesson: The maths of security decisions
Independent events, all happen
P(A and B) = P(A) × P(B)
Used for layers that must all fail.
Lesson: The maths of security decisions
Complement
P(not A) = 1 − P(A)
Often it is easier to work out the chance that nothing goes wrong, then subtract from 1.
Lesson: The maths of security decisions
Gordon and Loeb boundExam favourite
best spend ≤ (1 / e) × expected loss ≈ 0.368 × expected loss
For the classes of breach probability functions studied by Gordon and Loeb (2002). A rule of thumb, not a law.

Tips and traps

  • Remember
    Cybersecurity is not only about hackers in hoodies. Most damage comes from ordinary gaps: a missed update, a reused password, a public storage folder, or a person tricked by a fake email.Lesson: What cybersecurity is
  • In the exam
    Goals can pull against each other. Locking a file with a strong password raises confidentiality but can hurt availability if the password is lost. Good security balances all three for each asset.Lesson: The three goals: confidentiality, integrity and availability
  • Common mistake
    A vulnerability is the weakness, an exploit is the method that uses it, and a threat is who might use it. Students often swap these three in exams.Lesson: The words every security person uses
  • Remember
    Attackers usually pick the easiest way in, not the cleverest. That is why boring basics (updates, MFA, backups, training) stop most real attacks.Lesson: Who attacks and why: real incidents
  • In the exam
    The attacker needs to win once. The defender needs to win every time. Layers change this: now the attacker must win at every layer, every time.Lesson: Defence in depth: many layers, not one wall
  • In the exam
    Open design is also called Kerckhoffs’s principle (1883): a cipher should stay safe even if the enemy knows everything about it except the key. Hiding the method is called security through obscurity, and it is not enough on its own.Lesson: Design principles that last
  • Common mistake
    Exam details (number of questions, time, passing score, price) change every few years. Always check the official page of CompTIA, EC-Council, ISC2 or OffSec before you book. The revision chapter at the end of this subject lists them as checked when it was written.Lesson: Roles and the certification path
