1.1

What cybersecurity is: goals, threats and layers

Open chapter

the CIA triad, the words of security, attackers and real incidents, defence in depth with its maths, design principles, zero trust and careers.

Key ideas

  • Cybersecurity Protects systems and data from people who want to steal, change or stop them. People, process and technology together.
  • CIA triad Confidentiality (right people read), integrity (no unnoticed change), availability (works when needed). AAA: authentication, authorisation, accounting.
  • Words Asset, threat, vulnerability (weakness), exploit (method), risk = likelihood × impact, control, attack surface, zero day, CVE.
  • Defence in depth Independent layers: P(breach) = product of miss chances. Many tries: P(at least one) = 1 − (1 − p)n.
  • Principles Least privilege, fail safe defaults, complete mediation, economy of mechanism, open design (Kerckhoffs), separation of privilege, zero trust.
  • Money Expected loss = probability × cost. Gordon and Loeb: best spend under about 37% (1/e) of expected loss.

Authenticity: it really comes from who it says. Non repudiation: the sender cannot later deny sending it. Accountability: every action can be traced to a person.

Tips and traps

  • Remember
    Cybersecurity is not only about hackers in hoodies. Most damage comes from ordinary gaps: a missed update, a reused password, a public storage folder, or a person tricked by a fake email.Lesson: What cybersecurity is
  • In the exam
    Goals can pull against each other. Locking a file with a strong password raises confidentiality but can hurt availability if the password is lost. Good security balances all three for each asset.Lesson: The three goals: confidentiality, integrity and availability
  • Common mistake
    A vulnerability is the weakness, an exploit is the method that uses it, and a threat is who might use it. Students often swap these three in exams.Lesson: The words every security person uses
  • Remember
    Attackers usually pick the easiest way in, not the cleverest. That is why boring basics (updates, MFA, backups, training) stop most real attacks.Lesson: Who attacks and why: real incidents
  • In the exam
    The attacker needs to win once. The defender needs to win every time. Layers change this: now the attacker must win at every layer, every time.Lesson: Defence in depth: many layers, not one wall
  • In the exam
    Open design is also called Kerckhoffs’s principle (1883): a cipher should stay safe even if the enemy knows everything about it except the key. Hiding the method is called security through obscurity, and it is not enough on its own.Lesson: Design principles that last

    Exam details (number of questions, time, passing score, price) change every few years. Always check the official page of CompTIA, EC-Council, ISC2 or OffSec before you book. Risk in numbers: matrices, ALE, attack trees and continuity

Open chapter

risk matrices, SLE, ARO and ALE, risk responses and control types, STRIDE and attack trees, FAIR simulation, RTO, RPO and the nines.

Key ideas

  • Risk Risk = likelihood × impact. Inherent before controls, residual after. Appetite = what the business accepts.
  • Money SLE = AV × EF. ALE = SLE × ARO. Control value = ALEbefore − ALEafter − cost.
  • Responses and controls Avoid, reduce, transfer, accept. Categories: technical, managerial, operational, physical. Types: preventive, deterrent, detective, corrective, compensating, directive.
  • Threat models STRIDE: spoofing, tampering, repudiation, information disclosure, DoS, elevation of privilege. Attack trees: AND adds, OR takes the cheapest.
  • Uncertainty FAIR: risk = loss event frequency × loss magnitude. Monte Carlo gives the average and the 1 in 10 or 1 in 100 year loss.
  • Time RTO = time to restore, RPO = data you may lose. A = MTBF / (MTBF + MTTR). 99.9% = 8.76 h down per year.

Formulas

RiskExam favourite
risk = likelihood × impact
Likelihood: how often. Impact: how bad one event is. Both can be scores (1 to 5) or numbers (events per year, rupees).

residual risk = inherent risk − what the controls remove
Inherent risk is the risk before any control. Residual risk is what is left after controls. Some residual risk always stays.

SLE = AV × EF

Percentile of yearly loss

Downtime allowedExam favourite

The maths for security: bits, XOR, mod, counting, probability and entropy

Open chapter

binary and hex, XOR, modular arithmetic, key spaces, the birthday bound, the base rate trap and entropy.

Key ideas

  • Bits and hex Byte = 8 bits = 0 to 255 = 2 hex digits. n bits hold 2n values. Encoding (Base64, hex) is not encryption.
  • XOR 1 when bits differ. (m ⊕ k) ⊕ k = m. Same key twice leaks m1 ⊕ m2.
  • Mod a mod n = remainder. Reduce after every step. Caesar c = (m + k) mod 26.
  • Counting Key space NL, strength L log2 N bits. Each bit doubles brute force work.
  • Birthday and Bayes Collisions after about 2b/2 tries. P(attack | alarm) = TP / (TP + FP): rare attacks make most alarms false.
  • Entropy H = −Σ p log2 p. Max log2 N. Random or encrypted bytes are near 8 bits per byte.

1.1

Classical ciphers: Caesar, substitution, Vigenère and the one time pad

Open chapter

Caesar and mod 26, frequency analysis, Vigenère and the index of coincidence, the one time pad and perfect secrecy, unicity distance.

Key ideas

  • Words Plaintext, ciphertext, key, key space, brute force. Kerckhoffs: only the key is secret.
  • Caesar C = (P + k) mod 26, P = (C − k) mod 26, A = 0. 26 keys. ROT13 is its own inverse.
  • Frequency analysis Substitution keeps letter counts. χ2 = Σ (O − E)2 / E. E 12.7%, T 9.1%, A 8.2%.
  • Vigenère Ci = (Pi + Ki mod m) mod 26. IC = Σ n(n − 1) / N(N − 1): English 0.066, random 0.038.
  • One time pad c = m ⊕ k, random key as long as the message, once. P(M | C) = P(M). |K| ≥ |M|. Reuse gives m1 ⊕ m2.
  • Unicity distance U ≈ H(K) / D, D ≈ 3.2 bits per letter. 26! ≈ 288.4, so about 28 letters.

RSA: keys, proofs, signatures and attacks

Open chapter

Key ideas

  • Keys n = pq, φ = (p − 1)(q − 1), e = 65537 with gcd(e, φ) = 1, d = e−1 mod φ (or λ).
  • Use c = me mod n, m = cd mod n. Sign s = pad(H(M))d, verify se. 61, 53, 17, 2753: 65 to 2790.

Open chapter

Key ideas

  • Exchange A = ga, B = gb, K = Ba = Ab = gab. p = 23, g = 5, a = 6, b = 15: 8, 19, key 2.

Open chapter

Key ideas

  • Shamir s = f(0), degree k − 1 mod p, share (i, f(i)). Lagrange at 0: ∑ yi ∏ xj/(xj − xi). k − 1 shares: nothing. VSS checks shares.

  • Zero knowledge Complete, sound, ZK (simulator). Cave: 2−n. Schnorr: t = gr, s = r + cx, gs = t yc. Two answers extract x.

The web: sessions, origins, tokens and the OWASP Top 10

Open chapter

HTTP and cookies, session security, same origin policy and CORS, JWTs and OAuth, broken access control and the OWASP Top 10.

Key ideas

  • Web basics HTTP is stateless. Cookies (Montulli 1994) add state. The same origin policy (Netscape 1995) isolates sites. HTTPS protects transport only.

  • Memory safety In bounds, while alive, right type. C and C++ do not check. About 70% of serious bugs (Microsoft 2019, Chromium 2020). Morris 1988, Aleph One 1996.

  • Finding bugs ASan, UBSan, coverage guided fuzzing (AFL 2013, libFuzzer, OSS-Fuzz). Hardening flags: stack protector, FORTIFY_SOURCE, PIE, RELRO, CET.

Key ideas

  • Why DP Anonymisation fails: Sweeney 1997 (ZIP, birth date, sex, 87%), AOL 2006, Netflix 2008. Reconstruction (Dinur and Nissim 2003, Census 2010 tests). DP: Dwork, McSherry, Nissim, Smith 2006, Gödel Prize 2017, US Census 2020.
  • Definition P[M(D) ∈ S] ≤ eε P[M(D′) ∈ S] + δ for neighbours D, D′. Small ε strong. δ much smaller than 1/n. Posterior from 50% at most eε/(1 + eε).
  • Mechanisms Randomised response (Warner 1965): P(yes) = p/2 + 1/4, ε = ln 3. Laplace b = Δf/ε (count Δ = 1, std √2 b). Gaussian σ = Δ2√(2 ln(1.25/δ))/ε. Exponential P ∝ eεu/(2Δu).
  • Composition Sequential Σεi, parallel max, post processing free, group kε. Advanced composition grows like √k. Rényi DP (Mironov 2017) and moments accountant for tight budgets.
  • Sensitivity and clipping Count 1, clipped mean (U − L)/n, clipped gradient C. Clipping bounds each person’s influence so finite noise can hide it.
  • DP in ML DP-SGD (Abadi 2016): per example gradients, clip to C, add N(0, σ2C2), sampling q = B/N, accountant gives (ε, δ). DP-FedAvg (2018) user level. Local DP (RAPPOR, Apple) versus central DP (Census). Adv ≤ eε − 1.
