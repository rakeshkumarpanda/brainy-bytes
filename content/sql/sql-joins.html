Lesson 1 of 5

INNER JOIN: rows that match

An order row says customer_id 3. To print "Meera ordered on 11 June" we must put the order row and the customer row side by side. A join does that: it pairs rows from two tables whenever a condition, written after ON, is true.

Inner joinExam favourite
SELECT ... FROM a JOIN b ON b.a_id = a.id;
JOIN alone means INNER JOIN. Only pairs that satisfy ON are kept.
Qualify columns
o.id, c.id
When both tables have a column called id, write the table or alias in front, or the engine reports an ambiguous column.
Common mistake
An inner join loses rows that have no partner. Kabir, Arjun and Vikram never ordered, so they do not appear in the result above.
Lesson 2 of 5

LEFT, RIGHT and FULL joins: keeping the unmatched

"List every customer with their number of orders, even zero" needs the customers who have no orders. A LEFT JOIN keeps every row of the left table. Where no partner exists, the right side columns are filled with NULL.

JoinKeepsUnmatched side gets
INNERonly matching pairsnothing, rows dropped
LEFT (OUTER)every left rowNULLs in the right columns
RIGHT (OUTER)every right rowNULLs in the left columns
FULL (OUTER)every row from both sidesNULLs on whichever side is missing
CROSSevery possible pair, no ONnot applicable
In the exam
SQLite supports RIGHT JOIN and FULL OUTER JOIN since version 3.39.0 (July 2022). MySQL still has no FULL OUTER JOIN: people write a LEFT JOIN UNION a RIGHT JOIN instead.
Lesson 3 of 5

Three tables, self joins and CROSS JOIN

Real questions often cross three or four tables. Each JOIN adds one more table and one more ON condition. "Which customer bought which product" needs customers, orders, order_items and products.

A self join joins a table to itself. In the company database each employee has a manager_id that points at another employee. Two aliases of the same table let us show each person next to their manager.

Common mistake
A join with a missing or wrong ON condition can turn into an accidental cross join: 10 000 rows times 10 000 rows is 100 million rows. If a result is suddenly huge, check the ON clauses first.
Lesson 4 of 5

ON versus WHERE, USING and NATURAL

For an inner join it makes no difference whether a filter sits in ON or WHERE. For an outer join it changes everything. A condition in ON decides which rows match. A condition in WHERE runs after the join and can throw away the NULL padded rows you wanted to keep.

ShortcutMeansAdvice
JOIN t USING (id)ON a.id = t.id, and the id column appears oncefine when both columns share a name
NATURAL JOIN tjoin on every column with the same nameavoid: adding a column later can silently change the join
FROM a, b WHERE a.x = b.xold comma style inner joinworks, but explicit JOIN ... ON is clearer
Lesson 5 of 5

From intuition to research

One idea, four depths

A join is matching cards from two piles. Inner keeps pairs, left keeps every card from the left pile.

Logically a join is a cross product followed by a filter on the ON condition. Outer joins then add back unmatched rows padded with NULL.

Engines run joins with three main algorithms: nested loop (for each left row look up the right side, fast with an index), hash join (build a hash table of the smaller side, best for big equality joins) and merge join (walk two sorted inputs together). SQLite uses nested loops, often with an automatic temporary index.

Choosing the order of many joins is hard: the number of possible orders grows faster than exponentially. Optimizers use dynamic programming for a handful of tables and heuristics or genetic search beyond that (PostgreSQL switches to its genetic optimizer at 12 tables by default).

Going deeper: Semi join and anti join

A semi join keeps left rows that have at least one match, without repeating them: that is what EXISTS and IN do. An anti join keeps left rows with no match: NOT EXISTS or LEFT JOIN ... WHERE right.key IS NULL. Optimizers often turn subqueries into these joins.
