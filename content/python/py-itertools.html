Lesson 1 of 12

The iterator toolbox

Source: Python 3.14 documentation of itertools (with its recipes), functools, operator, math.comb and math.perm.

What it is
itertools is a module of fast building blocks that take iterators and give back new iterators: endless counters, slicing, joining, grouping and every kind of combination. functools has tools that work on functions: reduce, partial, singledispatch and caches. operator gives the + , [ ] and . operators as ready made functions.
Why we need it
Loops that join lists, number items, group rows, try every pair or keep a running total are written again and again, and each hand written loop is a chance for an off by one bug. These modules are tested, written in C, lazy (they keep memory small) and they make the intention clear: product(sizes, colours) says more than two nested loops.
How it works
Each itertools function returns an iterator, so the tools snap together: chain them, slice them with islice, feed them to sum or list. functools.partial fixes some arguments of a function, reduce folds a list into one value, and singledispatch picks a version of a function by the type of its argument.

Common mistake
count, cycle and repeat never end on their own. Always stop them: with islice, zip with something finite, takewhile, or a break.

In the exam
singledispatch chose the int version for 21, the list version for [1, 2] and the base version for 2.5. It looks at the type of the FIRST argument only. For methods use functools.singledispatchmethod.
Remember
lru_cache(maxsize=2) keeps the 2 most recently used answers. Calling 1, 2, 1, 3, 1 gave 2 hits: when 3 arrived, 2 was the least recently used and was thrown away.

The maths: counting, prefix sums and folds

product(items, repeat=r)Exam favourite
nr
3 items, r = 2: 32 = 9 tuples.
permutations(items, r)Exam favourite
P(n, r) = n! / (n − r)!
4 items, r = 2: 24 / 2 = 12.
combinations(items, r)Exam favourite
C(n, r) = n! / (r! (n − r)!)
4 items, r = 2: 24 / (2 × 2) = 6.
combinations_with_replacement(items, r)
C(n + r − 1, r)
3 items, r = 2: C(4, 2) = 6.
Pascal's rule
C(n, r) = C(n − 1, r − 1) + C(n − 1, r)
C(4, 2) = C(3, 1) + C(3, 2) = 3 + 3 = 6: tuples with the first item, plus tuples without it.
Prefix sums with accumulateExam favourite
P = accumulate(a, initial=0), sum of a[i] to a[j] = P[j + 1] − P[i]
a = [3, 1, 4, 1, 5], P = [0, 3, 4, 8, 9, 14], a[1] + a[2] + a[3] = 9 − 3 = 6.
reduce is a left fold
reduce(f, [a, b, c]) = f(f(a, b), c)
reduce(operator.sub, [10, 3, 2]) = (10 − 3) − 2 = 5.
Worked example: 4 digit PINs with no digit used twice
  1. The first digit has 10 choices.
  2. The second has 9 left, the third 8 and the fourth 7.
  3. This is P(10, 4) = 10! / 6!.
  4. 10 × 9 × 8 × 7 = 5040, the same as the brute force count
Lesson 10 of 12

From beginner to expert

One idea, four depths

Use count, cycle, repeat and islice, chain lists together and keep running totals with accumulate.

Use product, permutations and combinations, group with sorted then groupby, and sort with itemgetter and attrgetter.

Use partial, reduce, singledispatch and lru_cache, and build your own tools such as sliding_window from the recipes.

Know the costs: combinatoric tools grow like nr or n!, tee buffers the gap between its copies, groupby shares one underlying iterator so each group must be used before the next. Choose between generating and counting with math.comb, and use prefix sums to answer range queries in O(1).

Beginner
Use the endless and joining tools.
Intermediate
Group, combine and sort with key functions.
Expert
Build recipes and reason about their cost.

Lesson 12 of 12

Chapter summary

  1. count, cycle and repeat are endless: stop them with islice, zip or takewhile.
  2. takewhile and dropwhile cut a sequence at the first failure. filter and compress check every item.
  3. chain joins, zip_longest pairs to the longest, pairwise gives neighbours, batched gives groups, accumulate gives running results.
  4. groupby joins only neighbours with equal keys, so sort by the same key first.
  5. product nr, permutations n!/(n − r)!, combinations C(n, r), with replacement C(n + r − 1, r). Count first with math.comb and math.perm.
  6. functools: reduce folds left, partial fixes arguments, singledispatch picks by type, lru_cache keeps recent answers.
  7. operator: itemgetter, attrgetter and methodcaller are fast, clear key functions.
