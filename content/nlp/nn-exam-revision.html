Lesson 1 of 6

How the neural NLP part is examined

This chapter is for the week before an exam. It does not teach new material. It shows the shapes questions take at each level, with worked answers, and points back to the chapter where each idea is taught. The questions are original practice questions in the style of each tier, not copies of past papers.

The course quiz format, as announced in class: about 10 questions in 20 minutes, MCQ (one correct option) and MSQ (several correct options) plus numerical questions. A calculator is allowed, the formulas are given, and there is negative marking. The instructor said questions come from the steps of neural networks, word embeddings, the co-occurrence matrix and GloVe, so know each algorithm step by step.

Tier 1: school and first year

Questions at this level check that you can apply one formula and explain an idea with an everyday example.

SigmoidExam favourite
σ(z) = 11 + e−z
Squashes any number into 0 to 1.
Cosine similarityExam favourite
cos(a, b) = a · b‖a‖ ‖b‖
1 same direction, 0 unrelated, −1 opposite.
Gradient descentExam favourite
w ← w − η ∂J∂w
Step downhill by the learning rate times the slope.
Question 1. Why can one-hot vectors not show that "hotel" and "motel" are similar?
  1. Each word has a 1 in its own position and 0 elsewhere.
  2. The dot product of two different one-hot vectors is always 0.
  3. So every pair of different words is equally unrelated. Dense word vectors fix this.
Question 2. Cosine similarity of (1, 2) and (2, 4).
  1. Dot product 1 × 2 + 2 × 4 = 10.
  2. Lengths √5 and √20, product √100 = 10.
  3. cos = 10 ÷ 10 = 1, the same direction
Question 3. J(w) = (w − 3)², w = 0, η = 0.1. Find w after one step.
  1. Slope dJ/dw = 2(w − 3) = −6.
  2. w ← 0 − 0.1 × (−6).
  3. w = 0.6, a step towards the minimum at 3
Question 4. Turn "Sachin Tendulkar played for India" into BIO tags with PER and GPE.
  1. Sachin begins a person: B-PER. Tendulkar continues it: I-PER.
  2. played and for are outside: O, O.
  3. India: B-GPE

Short answer favourites: what a language model does (chapter 3.1), why "the cat" beats "a cat" in the beam example (chapter 4.3), what the forget gate does (chapter 3.3), and what "you shall know a word by the company it keeps" means (chapter 2.1).

Common mistake
Do not confuse similarity with relatedness. "cup" and "coffee" are related (they appear together) but not similar (one is a container, one is a drink).
Lesson 3 of 6

Tier 2: university semester and IIT course exams

Here the examiner gives sizes, vectors or counts and asks you to compute by hand. Write the formula, substitute, and state the meaning of the answer.

Question 5. Skip-gram with V = 6,000, d = 200, window 2. (a) one-hot size (b) hidden size (c) training pairs from an 8 word sentence.
  1. (a) The one-hot input has V = 6,000 entries.
  2. (b) The hidden layer is the embedding, 200 numbers.
  3. (c) Middle words have 4 neighbours, edge words fewer: 2 + 3 + 4 × 4 + 3 + 2.
  4. = 26 training pairs
Question 6. Co-occurrence with window 1 on "I like NLP . I like deep learning .". Find X(I, like) and X(like, NLP).
  1. Treat each sentence separately. "I like" appears in both sentences.
  2. X(I, like) = 2 with a symmetric window.
  3. X(like, NLP) = 1 from the first sentence.
  4. X(I, like) = 2, X(like, NLP) = 1
Question 7. GloVe with xmax = 100, α = 0.75. Compute f(10) and f(150).
  1. f(10) = (10 ÷ 100)0.75 = 0.10.75.
  2. f(10) = 0.1778
  3. f(150) = 1 because 150 ≥ xmax
Question 8. Dot-product attention: encoder states [1, 0] and [0, 1], decoder state [2, 0]. Context vector.
  1. Scores 2 and 0.
  2. Weights e² ÷ (e² + 1) = 0.8808 and 0.1192.
  3. context = 0.8808 [1, 0] + 0.1192 [0, 1] = [0.8808, 0.1192]
In the exam
Units and assumptions earn marks. Write "excluding biases", "natural log", "window counted on both sides" next to the answer.
Lesson 4 of 6

Tier 3: MCQ, MSQ and numerical answer papers

These papers reward speed and precision. With negative marking, a blind guess is not free. With no partial credit in MSQ, every ticked option must be right.

Expected mark of a guessExam favourite
E = p · (+1) + (1 − p) · (−m)
p chance of being right, m the penalty per wrong answer.
Question 9. Four options, +1 for right, −1/3 for wrong. Is a blind guess worth it? And after ruling out one option?
  1. Blind: p = 1/4, E = 1/4 − 3/4 × 1/3 = 0. No gain on average.
  2. One option ruled out: p = 1/3, E = 1/3 − 2/3 × 1/3 = 1/9.
  3. Guess only when you can eliminate at least one option
Question 10 (MSQ). Which are true of negative sampling? (A) it replaces the softmax with sigmoids (B) noise words come from U(w)3/4 (C) the true context can be a negative (D) only 1 + K output vectors change per pair.
  1. (A) True, binary logistic regressions.
  2. (B) True, the 3/4 power.
  3. (C) False, the notebook skips the true context word.
  4. Answer: A, B and D
Question 11 (numerical). Window 2, K = 5, full window. Sigmoid computations per centre word?
  1. 4 context words.
  2. Each: 1 positive + 5 negatives = 6.
  3. 4 × 6 = 24

Lesson 5 of 6

Tier 4: derivations, design and critique

Research university exams ask why, not only what. Expect to derive a gradient, justify a design, and say when a method fails.

Derivation 1. Softmax with cross-entropy gives the gradient ŷ − y.
  1. J = −log ŷc with ŷk = ezk ÷ ∑j ezj, so J = −zc + log ∑j ezj.
  2. ∂J/∂zk = −[k = c] + ezk ÷ ∑j ezj = ŷk − yk.
  3. Example: ŷ = [0.1, 0.6, 0.2, 0.1], gold 2. Gradient = [0.1, −0.4, 0.2, 0.1].
  4. ∂J/∂z = ŷ − y: push the gold score up, the others down in proportion
Derivation 2. Why does a plain RNN forget, and why does the LSTM cell help?
  1. ∂h(t)/∂h(j) = ∏ diag(tanh′) Wh, so its size is at most (‖Wh‖)t−j for tanh.
  2. If ‖Wh‖ < 1 this shrinks exponentially: the gradient vanishes.
  3. For the LSTM, c(t) = f ⊙ c(t−1) + i ⊙ c̃ gives ∂c(t)/∂c(t−1) ≈ diag(f).
  4. The factor is a learned gate, near 1 when memory should be kept
Derivation 3. From probability ratios to the GloVe model.
  1. Want F((wi − wj)·w̃k) = P(k | i) ÷ P(k | j).
  2. Choose F = exp, then wi·w̃k = log P(k | i) = log Xik − log Xi.
  3. Absorb log Xi into bi and add b̃k for symmetry.
  4. wi·w̃k + bi + b̃k = log Xik, fitted by weighted least squares
Design question. You must translate between two low-resource languages with 20,000 parallel sentences. Propose a system and critique it.
  1. Start from a multilingual pre-trained model (transfer learning) rather than training from scratch.
  2. Add back translated data from monolingual text in the target language.
  3. Decode with beam search (k about 5) and length normalisation.
  4. Risks: synthetic data copies the errors of the back translation model, and BLEU on a tiny test set is noisy. Report confidence intervals.
One idea, four depths

At school level, explain ideas with examples and do one-step sums: a sigmoid, a cosine, a single gradient step.

At university level, compute by hand: parameter counts, co-occurrence entries, GloVe weights, attention weights, perplexity. Show every step.

In competitive papers, recognise the formula fast, avoid traps around log bases, BIO counts and shared weights, and guess only when an option is ruled out.

At research level, derive gradients, explain why gating and attention fix gradient flow, and critique designs with their failure modes and evaluation risks.
