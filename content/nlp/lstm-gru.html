Vanishing and exploding gradients

To learn that a word at step 1 matters for a prediction at step 20, the gradient must travel back through 19 steps. Each step multiplies it by the same kind of factor. If that factor is smaller than 1 the gradient shrinks towards 0 (vanishing). If it is bigger than 1 it blows up (exploding).

One step backExam favourite
∂h(t)∂h(t−1) = diag(σ′(Whh(t−1) + Wxx(t) + b)) Wh
The Jacobian of one RNN step.
Many steps backExam favourite
∂J(t)∂h(j) = ∂J(t)∂h(t) ∏j < k ≤ t ∂h(k)∂h(k−1)
A product of t − j Jacobians.
Size bound
‖∂h(t) ÷ ∂h(j)‖ ≤ (γ ‖Wh‖)t−j
γ bounds σ′ (1 for tanh). Below 1 the bound shrinks exponentially.
Effect on an RNN language model
  1. "When she tried to print her tickets, she found that the printer was out of toner. She went to the stationery store to buy more toner. It was very overpriced. After installing the toner into the printer, she finally printed her ___"
  2. The answer "tickets" depends on a word about 30 steps back.
  3. If the gradient from step 30 to step 1 is close to 0, the model cannot learn this dependency.
  4. RNN language models learn nearby effects well and long range effects poorly

Keeps the direction, limits the step size.
Common mistake
Vanishing gradients mean the model does not learn long range dependencies. They do not mean the loss is 0 or that training stops at once. Exploding gradients show up as NaN losses.
Lesson 2 of 5

Long Short-Term Memory

Hochreiter and Schmidhuber (1997) gave the RNN a separate memory cell c(t), the horizontal line on top of the diagram. Three gates, each a vector of numbers between 0 and 1, decide what to erase, what to write and what to show. A gate value of 1 means open, 0 means closed.

Forget gateExam favourite
f(t) = σ(Wfh(t−1) + Ufx(t) + bf)
What to keep from the old cell.
Input gate
i(t) = σ(Wih(t−1) + Uix(t) + bi)
How much of the new content to write.
Output gate
o(t) = σ(Woh(t−1) + Uox(t) + bo)
How much of the cell to show as h.
New cell content
c̃(t) = tanh(Wch(t−1) + Ucx(t) + bc)
The candidate to write.
Cell stateExam favourite
c(t) = f(t) ⊙ c(t−1) + i(t) ⊙ c̃(t)
Erase some, write some. ⊙ is element-wise.
Hidden stateExam favourite
h(t) = o(t) ⊙ tanh(c(t))
What the next layer and the next step see.
One cell update by hand (the widget start values)
  1. c(t−1) = 0.8, candidate c̃ = −0.5, f = 0.9, i = 0.2, o = 0.7.
  2. Keep: 0.9 × 0.8 = 0.72. Write: 0.2 × (−0.5) = −0.10.
  3. c(t) = 0.72 − 0.10 = 0.62
  4. h(t) = 0.7 × tanh(0.62) = 0.7 × 0.5511 = 0.3858
LSTM layer parametersExam favourite
4 · H · (E + H) + 4H
Four blocks (f, i, o, c̃), each with an H × E input matrix, an H × H recurrent matrix and a bias.
Real world scenario: remembering the subject of a long sentence

In "The keys to the old wooden cabinet in the hall are on the table", the verb must agree with "keys", eight words back. An LSTM can write "subject is plural" into one cell dimension, keep its forget gate near 1 while the cabinet phrase goes by, and read it out at the verb. A plain RNN tends to let the nearer word "hall" win.

Lesson 3 of 5

How the LSTM keeps gradients alive

The key is the additive update of the cell. From c(t) = f ⊙ c(t−1) + i ⊙ c̃, the direct path from the old cell to the new one is multiplied only by the forget gate, with no squashing function and no shared weight matrix in between.

Direct cell pathExam favourite
∂c(t)∂c(t−1) ≈ diag(f(t))
Ignoring the paths through the gates. With f = 1 the cell is a straight wire.
Over many steps
∏k f(k)
The network learns f. Near 1 it keeps information for many steps, near 0 it forgets on purpose.
RNNLSTM
memoryh(t), rewritten every stepseparate cell c(t), changed only through gates
gradient through one stepWh times tanh′f, learned per step
to keep a factweights must be tuned just right, which is very hardset the forget gate to 1 and the input gate to 0

The slide summary: if the forget gate tells the model to remember everything, the memory inside the LSTM hardly changes and the information can stay for many time steps. A normal RNN has no special memory control, so important information often fades as time passes.

In the exam
An LSTM does not guarantee there is no vanishing or exploding gradient. It makes it much easier for the model to learn long range dependencies. In practice it handles about 100 steps well where a plain RNN manages about 7.
Going deeper: residual connections are the same idea

ResNets add x to the output of a block: y = x + F(x), so ∂y/∂x = I + ∂F/∂x always contains an identity path. The LSTM cell with f = 1 is that identity path through time. Highway networks (Srivastava et al. 2015) made the link explicit with a learned gate between the identity and the transformation.

Lesson 4 of 5

Gated Recurrent Units

Cho and others (2014) proposed a simpler gated unit with no separate cell. The hidden state holds both short and long term memory. An update gate decides how much new information replaces the old memory, and a reset gate decides how much of the past is used to build the new candidate.

Update gateExam favourite
zt = σ(Wzxt + Uzht−1 + bz)
How much new information to store.
Reset gate
rt = σ(Wrxt + Urht−1 + br)
How much of the past to forget when building the candidate.
Candidate
h̃t = tanh(Whxt + Uh(rt ⊙ ht−1) + bh)
New information from the input and the filtered past.
New stateExam favourite
ht = (1 − zt) ⊙ ht−1 + zt ⊙ h̃t
A blend of old and new. z = 0 keeps the old state exactly.
One GRU update by hand
  1. ht−1 = 0.8, candidate h̃ = −0.5, update gate z = 0.3.
  2. Old part: (1 − 0.3) × 0.8 = 0.56.
  3. New part: 0.3 × (−0.5) = −0.15.
  4. ht = 0.56 − 0.15 = 0.41

Remember
In a GRU, (1 − z) plays the role of the LSTM forget gate and z the role of the input gate. Tying them means writing new information always erases the same amount of old information.
Lesson 5 of 5

BiLSTM, the parameter question and xLSTM

A BiLSTM runs one LSTM forwards and another backwards and joins their states, exactly like the BiRNN of chapter 3.2. The lecture asks for its parameter count in a language identification task for code-switched text, where each word must be tagged with its language, as in "main kal market jaaunga to buy vegetables".

The BiLSTM question
  1. Embedding 60, hidden 32 per direction, 4 language tags, no biases.
  2. One LSTM direction: 4 × 32 × (60 + 32) = 4 × 32 × 92 = 11,776.
  3. Two directions: 2 × 11,776 = 23,552.
  4. Output layer on the joined 64 numbers: 64 × 4 = 256.
  5. Total = 23,552 + 256 = 23,808

Recurrent models have two deeper issues. Information between distant words must pass through every step in between (linear interaction distance). And step t cannot start before step t − 1 finishes, so the computation cannot be parallelised over the sequence, which inhibits training on very large datasets. These issues led to attention and transformers.

One idea, four depths

A plain RNN forgets things quickly. An LSTM has a notebook and three switches: erase, write and show. Keeping the erase switch off lets it remember for a long time.

LSTM: c = f ⊙ cold + i ⊙ c̃, h = o ⊙ tanh(c). GRU: h = (1 − z) ⊙ hold + z ⊙ h̃. Parameters 4H(E + H) and 3H(E + H), doubled for a bidirectional model.

The additive cell path gives ∂c(t)/∂c(t−1) ≈ diag(f), a learned and input dependent factor instead of a fixed Wh times tanh′. This is the same identity path idea as residual connections.

xLSTM revisits the LSTM with exponential gates and a matrix memory. State space models such as Mamba also keep a recurrent state but train in parallel, which reopens the question of whether attention is needed at every layer.
