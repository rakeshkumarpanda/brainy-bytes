Lesson 1 of 4

Joint and conditional entropy

Language depends on history, so one variable at a time is not enough. We need the uncertainty of two variables together, and the uncertainty of one when the other is known. The professor's slide summed it up: language modelling is fundamentally about conditional probability.

Joint entropy
H(X, Y) = −∑x,y p(x, y) log₂ p(x, y)
The information needed on average to specify both values, like "New" and "Delhi" together.
Conditional entropyExam favourite
H(Y | X) = ∑x p(x) H(Y | X = x) = −∑x,y p(x, y) log₂ p(y | x)
The extra information still needed for Y once X is known. The joint probability goes outside the log, the conditional inside.
Chain ruleExam favourite
H(X, Y) = H(X) + H(Y | X)
Specify X, then specify Y given X. For n variables: H(X₁ … Xₙ) = Σ H(Xᵢ | X₁ … Xᵢ₋₁).
Common mistake
A student guessed −Σ p(x,y) log p(x|y). That is not H(Y|X), but it is a correct formula for H(X|Y): the conditioning just runs the other way. In the umbrella example it gives 0.593 bits against 0.621 for H(Y|X).

The weather and the umbrella

X is the weather (sunny 0.6, rainy 0.4). Y is whether I carry an umbrella: probability 0.2 on a sunny day and 0.9 on a rainy day.

All the entropies, step by step
  1. Joint probabilities, multiplying along the branches: P(S, U) = 0.12, P(S, N) = 0.48, P(R, U) = 0.36, P(R, N) = 0.04.
  2. Marginals: P(U) = 0.12 + 0.36 = 0.48. So H(Y) = H(0.48) = 0.999 bits, almost a fair coin. H(X) = H(0.6) = 0.971 bits.
  3. Given sunny: H(0.2) = 0.722. Given rainy: H(0.9) = 0.469.
  4. H(Y|X) = 0.6 × 0.722 + 0.4 × 0.469 = 0.433 + 0.188 = 0.621 bits
  5. Joint: H(X, Y) = −(0.12 log 0.12 + 0.48 log 0.48 + 0.36 log 0.36 + 0.04 log 0.04) = 1.592 bits.
  6. Chain rule check: 0.971 + 0.621 = 1.592, which matches
  7. Knowing the weather cut the umbrella uncertainty from 0.999 to 0.621 bits, a saving of 0.378 bits.

Does conditioning always reduce entropy?

On average, yes: H(Y | X) ≤ H(Y), with equality only when X and Y are independent. For one particular value it can go up. If P(X = a) = 0.9 and Y is then always y₁, while X = b makes Y a fair coin, then H(Y) = H(0.95) = 0.286 bits but H(Y | X = b) = 1 bit. The average H(Y | X) = 0.9 × 0 + 0.1 × 1 = 0.1 bits is still lower. Brown shows the same: after "of" the next word has 8.62 bits, but after "of the" it has 11.02 bits, because once "the" is used almost any noun can follow.

The chain rule for a sentence
  1. P(John went to Patna) = P(John) · P(went | John) · P(to | John went) · P(Patna | John went to).
  2. Take −log₂: the total surprise is the sum of the four surprises.
  3. With model probabilities 0.001, 0.05, 0.4 and 0.01 the surprises are 9.97 + 4.32 + 1.32 + 6.64.
  4. Total = 22.25 bits, and 0.001 × 0.05 × 0.4 × 0.01 = 2 × 10⁻⁷ = 2−22.25, which matches
Lesson 2 of 4

Mutual information

A student called it "common information", and that is the best everyday description. Mutual information is how much knowing one variable reduces the uncertainty about the other.

Mutual informationExam favourite
I(X : Y) = H(Y) − H(Y | X) = H(X) + H(Y) − H(X, Y)
The overlap of the two entropy circles, in bits. Many textbooks put a semicolon between X and Y in this notation.
Probability form
I(X : Y) = ∑x,y p(x, y) log₂ p(x, y)p(x) p(y)
Zero exactly when p(x, y) = p(x) p(y) for every pair, which is independence.

Three facts and their proofs

  • Independent means zero. If p(x, y) = p(x)p(y), every ratio inside the log is 1 and log 1 = 0. The class answered "zero, zero, zero".
  • Symmetric. p(x, y) is the same number as p(y, x) and p(x)p(y) equals p(y)p(x), so swapping X and Y changes no term. I(X : Y) = I(Y : X).
  • Never negative. The probability form is a KL divergence, which is never negative (next lesson). So H(Y | X) ≤ H(Y) on average.
Studying and passing
  1. 60% of students study. Of those, 90% pass. Of the rest, 20% pass.
  2. Joint: P(study, pass) = 0.54, P(study, fail) = 0.06, P(no study, pass) = 0.08, P(no study, fail) = 0.32.
  3. Marginals: P(pass) = 0.62. H(X) = H(0.6) = 0.971, H(Y) = H(0.62) = 0.958, H(X, Y) = 1.541.
  4. I(X : Y) = 0.971 + 0.958 − 1.541 = 0.388 bits
  5. Cell by cell the four terms are +0.290, −0.116, −0.131 and +0.344. Single cells can be negative but the total cannot.

Word pairs: New Delhi

Seeing "New" sharply raises the chance that the next word is "Delhi". For one specific pair we use the pointwise version.

Pointwise mutual informationExam favourite
PMI(x, y) = log₂ p(x, y)p(x) p(y) = log₂ c(x y) · Nc(x) · c(y)
Above 0 the words attract, 0 means independent, below 0 they avoid each other. MI is the average of PMI over all pairs.

On real Brown counts, c(new) = 1,635, c(york) = 302 and c(new york) = 296 of N = 991,429 give PMI = 9.22 bits, and 98% of all "york" tokens follow "new".

Common mistake
PMI over rewards rare pairs. Two words seen once, together, score log₂ N, more than New Delhi. Always use a minimum count, or a t-score or log likelihood ratio, alongside it.

KL divergence and cross entropy

Mutual information compares two variables. Relative entropy compares two probability distributions over the same events. A student gave the right intuition: a model built on New York data, used in India, wastes bits because it expects the wrong things.

Kullback Leibler divergenceExam favourite
D(p ‖ q) = ∑x p(x) log₂ p(x)q(x)
The average number of bits wasted by coding data from p with a code built for q.
Cross entropyExam favourite
H(p, q) = −∑x p(x) log₂ q(x) = H(p) + D(p ‖ q)
Training a language model with cross entropy loss lowers the KL from the truth to the model, since H(p) is fixed by the data.
Gibbs' inequality: D(p ‖ q) ≥ 0
  1. Use ln z ≤ z − 1 for z > 0, with equality only at z = 1. The line z − 1 touches ln z at z = 1 and lies above it.
  2. Put z = q(x)/p(x) and sum over x with p(x) > 0: Σ p ln(q/p) ≤ Σ p (q/p − 1) = Σ q − Σ p ≤ 1 − 1 = 0.
  3. The left side is −D(p ‖ q) × ln 2, so D(p ‖ q) ≥ 0.
  4. Equality needs q/p = 1 everywhere, that is p = q.
  5. Consequences: I(X : Y) ≥ 0, H(Y | X) ≤ H(Y), and no model has lower cross entropy than the truth.
ExampleD(p ‖ q)D(q ‖ p)

In the exam
If a model gives probability 0 to something that happens, KL and perplexity become infinite. That single fact is why every n-gram model in chapter 4 is smoothed, and why neural models use a softmax, which never outputs exactly 0.
Lesson 4 of 4

The noisy channel

Shannon's model of communication has a sender, an encoder, a noisy channel and a decoder. Engineers design the encoder to add just enough redundancy to correct errors. In language we cannot design the encoder, since people type and speak as they do. We can only decode: given what came out, find the most likely thing that went in.

Noisy channel decodingExam favourite
î = argmaxi p(i | o) = argmaxi p(i) · p(o | i)
p(i) is the language model (how likely is this input). p(o | i) is the channel model (how likely is this output from it). Used for spelling correction, speech recognition, OCR and translation.
Channel capacity
C = maxp(X) I(X : Y)
A binary channel that flips 10% of bits has C = 1 − H(0.1) = 0.531 bits per use.

Why can p(o) be dropped? Bayes gives p(i | o) = p(i) p(o | i) ÷ p(o). The observation is fixed while we compare candidates, so p(o) is the same positive number for all of them and cannot change which is largest.

Correcting "acress"
  1. Candidates with P(word) and P(acress | word), in the style of Kernighan, Church and Gale (1990): actress 1.4e-5 and 1.2e-4, across 2.9e-4 and 2.1e-6, acres 3.2e-5 and 3.1e-5, access 1.8e-4 and 1.0e-6.
  2. Products: actress 1.68e-9, across 6.09e-10, acres 9.92e-10, access 1.80e-10.
  3. Winner: actress, with share 1.68 ÷ 3.46 ≈ 0.485
  4. across is a far more common word, but turning across into acress needs an unlikely change, so its channel probability is small.
Real world scenario: voice typing an address

A delivery app hears "I will be back soonish" and "I will be bassoon dish", which sound almost the same. The acoustic model (the channel) scores both about equally. The language model knows the first is a normal sentence and the second is not, so the product picks the right one. This is the same argmax as the spelling example.

One idea, four depths

Knowing one thing can tell you about another: the weather tells you about umbrellas, "New" tells you about "Delhi". Mutual information measures how much.

H(X,Y) = H(X) + H(Y|X), I(X : Y) = H(Y) − H(Y|X), PMI scores single word pairs, D(p‖q) ≥ 0 measures wasted bits, and the noisy channel picks argmax p(i) p(o|i).

MI equals D(p(x,y) ‖ p(x)p(y)), which proves non negativity and subadditivity H(X,Y) ≤ H(X) + H(Y). Cross entropy loss equals entropy plus KL, so minimising it is maximum likelihood. Channel capacity is the largest MI over input distributions.

Contrastive losses such as InfoNCE bound mutual information, I ≥ log N − L (Oord et al., 2018), which links CLIP style training to this chapter. Research studies estimating MI in high dimensions, the asymmetry of forward and reverse KL in model distillation, and noisy channel reranking in neural translation.
