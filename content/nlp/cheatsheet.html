1.1

What language is

Open chapter

The working definition, six levels from phonetics to pragmatics, symbols and the semiotic triangle, the speaker and hearer chain, Zipf and Grice, and recursion.

Key ideas

  • Working definition Language is a systematic, symbolic and structured system that starts as thought, organises it, and lets it be rebuilt in another mind.
  • Six levels Phonetics, phonology, morphology, syntax, semantics, pragmatics. Each combines the units of the level below.
  • Two functions Symbolic: words stand for concepts, not directly for things (semiotic triangle). Interactive: a speaker encodes and a hearer reconstructs, never an exact copy.
  • Form and meaning A symbol is a conventional form meaning pair. Unicorn has a sense without a reference. TAM marks present, past and imagined reality.
  • Effort and clarity Zipf: least effort, f(r) ≈ C ÷ r. Grice: quantity, quality, relation, manner.
  • Recursion A clause inside a clause without limit. Everett claimed Pirahã lacks it, a challenge to Hauser, Chomsky and Fitch. Still debated.

Ambiguity: why language is hard for machines

Open chapter

Lexical against structural ambiguity, the Lesk method, PP attachment, Catalan growth of parse trees and how machines resolve ambiguity.

Key ideas

  • Two kinds Lexical: one word, several meanings (bank, over). Structural: one string, several trees (telescope).
  • Word types Homonymy (unrelated meanings), polysemy (related senses), homophones (same sound), homographs (same spelling).
  • Lesk Pick the sense whose gloss shares most words with the context. A counting version of Firth.
  • Catalan growth A verb, object and k prepositional phrases have C(k + 1) trees: 2, 5, 14, 42, 132.
  • Resolution Rules (right association), treebank statistics (lexical association), contextual neural models. Ask when the cost of a wrong guess is high.
  • Lecture examples I sat near a bank. The cat jumped over the wall. John saw Mary with a telescope. Understands you like your mother. Copy the local patient files.

Formulas

Simplified LeskExam favourite
sense* = argmaxs |context(w) ∩ gloss(s)|
Count the shared words between the sentence and each gloss, after removing very common words. Ties and zero overlaps fall back to the most frequent sense.
Lesson: Lexical ambiguity: one word, several meanings
Catalan numbersExam favourite
Cn = 1n + 1 × (2n choose n) = (2n)!(n + 1)! n!
With k phrases the count is Ck+1: 2, 5, 14, 42, 132 for k = 1 to 5.
Lesson: Structural ambiguity: one string, several trees
Lexical association for attachment
P(verb attachment) ≈ c(verb, prep)c(verb, prep) + c(noun, prep)
In the spirit of Hindle and Rooth (1993): compare how strongly the preposition goes with the verb and with the noun in a large corpus.
Lesson: How machines resolve ambiguity

Can machines understand language?

Open chapter

Marr's levels, Turing and Total Turing tests, ELIZA against SHRDLU, how NLU systems are built and scored, and the debate on form and meaning.

Key ideas

  • Marr Computational (what and why), algorithmic (how, representation), implementational (where). Design top down, validate bottom up.
  • Turing test Operational, text only, not reproducible. Qualia are outside it. The Total Turing Test adds perception and action, seven capabilities.
  • ELIZA and SHRDLU ELIZA: keyword patterns, no understanding. SHRDLU: world model, reasoning, action and clarifying questions in a blocks world.
  • Fields and pipeline NLP builds tools, CL models cognition, NLU is the shared goal. Corpus and annotation, model, then validation.
  • Metrics P = TP ÷ (TP + FP), R = TP ÷ (TP + FN), F1 = 2PR ÷ (P + R). BLEU = BP · exp(Σ w log p). Surprisal −log₂ P. Dependency length.
  • Form against meaning Firth and Shannon support data. The octopus test, Ettinger, symbol grounding and the Chinese room warn that form is not meaning.

Open chapter

Inflection and derivation, Porter stemming and the measure m, Snowball and Lancaster, WordNet lemmatisation, stem against lemma, and the precision and recall trade off.

Key ideas

  • Stem against lemma Stem: rule based chop, may not be a word (leaves → leav). Lemma: dictionary form, uses POS (leaves → leaf, children → child).
  • Porter measure Word = [C](VC)^m[V]. tree 0, trouble 1, troubles 2. y after a consonant is a vowel.
  • Notebook results Porter: sportingli, fairli, boy'. Snowball: sport, fair, boy. Exercise list differs only on alumnus.
  • Lemmatiser needs POS WordNetLemmatizer defaults to noun. loving stays loving, but with pos='v' it becomes love.
  • Errors and effect Over stemming: university, universe → univers. Under stemming: alumnus and alumni stay apart. Stemming raises recall, may lower precision.

3.1

Zipf's law and the shape of vocabulary

Open chapter

Zipf's law f = k/r, the Brown check, coverage by harmonic numbers, least effort, Mandelbrot and Heaps, and why random typing is also Zipfian.

Key ideas

  • Zipf's law f = k/r, f × r ≈ constant, slope −1 on log log axes. f_a/f_b = b/a, so the 50th word is 3 times the 150th.
  • Brown check the 69,971, of 36,412 (ratio 1.92). f × r stays between about 70,000 and 107,000. Real slope about −1.08.
  • Coverage Top n share = H_n/H_V, H_n ≈ ln n + 0.5772. V = 50,000: top 10 = 25.7%, top 100 = 45.5%.
  • Least effort Frequent words are short (law of abbreviation). Language balances speaker effort and listener robustness.
  • Monkey typing Random keys give a power law with α = 1 − ln(1 − s)/ln M (1.0116 for 27 keys) but no predictability.

Open chapter

Key ideas

  • Surprisal I(x) = −log₂ p(x). p = 1 gives 0 bits, each halving adds 1 bit. Bits, nats, hartleys.

Mutual information, KL and the noisy channel

Open chapter

Joint and conditional entropy, the chain rule, mutual information and PMI, KL divergence and cross entropy, and noisy channel decoding.

Key ideas

  • Joint and conditional H(Y|X) = Σ p(x) H(Y|X=x) = −Σ p(x,y) log p(y|x). Chain rule H(X,Y) = H(X) + H(Y|X). Umbrella: 0.971 + 0.621 = 1.592.
  • Mutual information I = H(Y) − H(Y|X) = H(X) + H(Y) − H(X,Y) = Σ p(x,y) log p(x,y)/(p(x)p(y)). Symmetric, ≥ 0, 0 iff independent. Umbrella 0.378.
  • PMI log₂ c(xy)N/(c(x)c(y)). New Delhi 8.64, new car 3.32. Collocations score highest. Rare pairs are over rewarded.
  • KL and cross entropy D(p‖q) = Σ p log p/q ≥ 0, not symmetric, infinite if q = 0 where p > 0. H(p,q) = H(p) + D(p‖q).
  • Noisy channel argmax p(i) p(o|i): language model times channel model. acress → actress (0.485). BSC 10%: C = 0.531.

Formulas

Joint entropy
H(X, Y) = −∑x,y p(x, y) log₂ p(x, y)
The information needed on average to specify both values, like "New" and "Delhi" together.

Conditional entropyExam favourite
H(Y | X) = ∑x p(x) H(Y | X = x) = −∑x,y p(x, y) log₂ p(y | x)
The extra information still needed for Y once X is known. The joint probability goes outside the log, the conditional inside.
Lesson: Joint and conditional entropy
Chain ruleExam favourite
H(X, Y) = H(X) + H(Y | X)
Specify X, then specify Y given X. For n variables: H(X₁ … Xₙ) = Σ H(Xᵢ | X₁ … Xᵢ₋₁).
Lesson: Joint and conditional entropy
Mutual informationExam favourite
I(X : Y) = H(Y) − H(Y | X) = H(X) + H(Y) − H(X, Y)
The overlap of the two entropy circles, in bits. Many textbooks put a semicolon between X and Y in this notation.

Probability form
I(X : Y) = ∑x,y p(x, y) log₂ p(x, y)p(x) p(y)
Zero exactly when p(x, y) = p(x) p(y) for every pair, which is independence.

Pointwise mutual informationExam favourite

Kullback Leibler divergenceExam favourite

Cross entropyExam favourite
H(p, q) = −∑x p(x) log₂ q(x) = H(p) + D(p ‖ q)

Noisy channel decodingExam favourite

Open chapter

FSAs, the HMM five tuple, the joint probability, validity checks, training by counting and enumeration.

Key ideas

  • FSA Five tuple (Q, Σ, q0, δ, F). Accept only if every symbol has an arrow and the machine ends in a final state. Sheep talk is /baa+!/.
  • HMM Five tuple (Q, A, O, B, π). Tags are hidden, words are observed. A probabilistic Moore machine: states emit words.

  • Training Count starts, tag bigrams and (tag, word) pairs, then divide by row totals. Zeros need smoothing.

Open chapter

Weights, bias, squared error, gradient descent, learning rate, partial derivatives and the chain rule.

Key ideas

  • Model and cost A model has knobs (weights w and bias b). The squared error cost C = ∑(ŷ − y)² scores a guess. For the lecture data w = 1, b = 1.37 gives 0.67.
  • Gradient descent Move each knob against its slope: θ ← θ − η ∂C/∂θ. Too large η overshoots or diverges, too small η is slow.
  • One knob example NN() = b, cost (b − 4)², slope 2(b − 4). The distance to 4 is multiplied by 1 − 2η each step, η = 0.5 lands in one step.
  • Partial derivatives ∂C/∂w = ∑2(wx + b − y)x and ∂C/∂b = ∑2(wx + b − y). The cost over (w, b) is a bowl with one minimum.
  • Chain rule For ŷ = tanh(wx + b): ∂L/∂w = 2(ŷ − y)(1 − ŷ²)x. A negative gradient raises w, but a large step can still raise the cost.

Evaluating and improving embeddings

Open chapter

Key ideas

  • Intrinsic and extrinsic Intrinsic: fast subtasks (relatedness with Spearman, analogy, categorisation with purity, selectional preference). Extrinsic: score of a real task.
  • Using pre-trained vectors Fixed (fewer parameters, no adaptation), fine-tuned (adapts, may forget), concatenated (both, bigger). No embedding wins on every test.
  • Subwords fastText: vw = sum of character n-gram vectors with marks < >, n = 3 to 6. "where" has 14 n-grams. Handles rare and unseen words.
  • Cross-lingual Map spaces with W, often orthogonal (Procrustes W = UVT). Anchors: a dictionary, identical strings (Artetxe) or none (adversarial, Zhang).
  • Sparsity and bias Sparse: add λ‖w‖₁. Debias: bias direction g from pairs, neutralise v′ = v − (v·g)g, then equalise. Bias is reduced, not removed.

Formulas

4.3

Decoding: greedy, beam search and sampling

Open chapter

Greedy and beam search, stopping and length normalisation, and temperature, top-k and top-p sampling.

Key ideas

  • Greedy Pick argmax at each step. Fast, cannot undo decisions. Slide tree: greedy "a cat" 0.20, optimal "the cat" 0.36.
  • Beam search Keep the k best hypotheses by ∑ log P, expand each, keep the top k again. k = 1 is greedy. Not guaranteed optimal, far cheaper than |V|T.
  • Stopping and length A hypothesis that emits <END> is set aside. Stop at time T or after n complete hypotheses. Pick the best by (1 ÷ t) ∑ log P.
  • Sampling Temperature softmax(s ÷ T): low T sharp, high T flat. Top-k keeps k words, top-p the smallest set with mass ≥ p. Adds diversity.
  • Trade off Greedy and beam are deterministic and can repeat in open ended text. Sampling trades some quality for diversity.
