How NLP is examined

This chapter is for the week before an exam. It does not teach new material. It shows the shapes questions take at each level, with worked answers, and points back to the chapter where each idea is taught. The questions are original practice questions written in the style of each tier, not copies of real past papers.

The professor of this course said three things plainly. Derivations will be examined: "if you know how to derive, you know how it works". Hand computation will be examined: given a word sequence and the emission, transition and start tables, you must find the most likely tag sequence. Definitions will be asked with examples: stem against lemma, why perplexities are only comparable with the same vocabulary, and why we never train on the test set.

Lesson 2 of 6

Tier 1: school and first year

Questions at this level check that you can count words, read a simple probability and explain an idea with an example.

Type token ratioExam favourite
TTR = number of typesnumber of tokens
Types are distinct words. Tokens are running words.
Zipf's lawExam favourite
f × r ≈ C
The word of rank r appears about C ÷ r times.
Entropy
H = −Σ p log₂ p
Average surprise in bits. A fair coin has 1 bit.
Question 1. Count tokens and types in "the cat sat on the mat".
  1. Tokens: the, cat, sat, on, the, mat, so 6.
  2. Types: the, cat, sat, on, mat, so 5, since the appears twice.
  3. TTR = 5 ÷ 6 ≈ 0.833.
Question 2. The commonest word in a corpus appears 60,000 times. By Zipf's law, about how often does the third commonest appear?
  1. f × r ≈ C, so C ≈ 60,000 × 1 = 60,000.
  2. For r = 3, f ≈ 60,000 ÷ 3.
  3. ≈ 20,000 times. Real counts wobble around this line, especially at the top ranks.
Question 3. Give the stem and the lemma of "studies" and of "better".
  1. A stemmer chops suffixes by rule: studies → studi, better → better.
  2. A lemmatiser looks words up: studies → study, better → good (as an adjective).
  3. A stem need not be a real word, a lemma always is.
Question 4. Four outcomes have probabilities 1/2, 1/4, 1/8 and 1/8. Find the entropy.
  1. Surprisals: 1, 2, 3 and 3 bits.
  2. H = 1/2 × 1 + 1/4 × 2 + 1/8 × 3 + 1/8 × 3.
  3. = 0.5 + 0.5 + 0.375 + 0.375 = 1.75 bits.

Short answer favourites: name the six levels of linguistic structure (chapter 1.1), give one lexical and one structural ambiguity (chapter 1.2), explain what the Turing test checks and what it misses (chapter 1.3), and say why "Mr. Smith arrived." is hard for a sentence splitter (chapter 2.1).

Common mistake
Do not mix tokens and types. "How many words" is ambiguous in an exam. Say which you counted.

Tier 2: university semester and IIT course exams

Here the examiner gives a toy corpus or a set of tables and asks you to work by hand. Show every step. The corpus below is the one used in the n-gram chapters.

Training corpus
<s> I am Sam </s>
<s> Sam I am </s>
<s> Sam I like </s>
<s> Sam I do like </s>
<s> do I like Sam </s>
Question 5. Find P(I like Sam) with a bigram MLE model, including </s>.
  1. P(I | <s>) = C(<s> I) ÷ C(<s>) = 1 ÷ 5 = 0.2.
  2. P(like | I) = 2 ÷ 5 = 0.4. P(Sam | like) = 1 ÷ 3. P(</s> | Sam) = 2 ÷ 5 = 0.4.
  3. 0.2 × 0.4 × 0.3333 × 0.4 = 0.010667
Question 6. Find the perplexity of that sentence.
  1. Predicted tokens: I, like, Sam and </s>, so N = 4. <s> is only context.
  2. PP = P−1/N = 0.010667−1/4.
  3. PP ≈ 3.112
Question 7. Redo P(I | <s>) with Laplace smoothing. The vocabulary has V = 6 types (I, am, Sam, like, do, </s>).
  1. PLap(w | h) = (C(h w) + 1) ÷ (C(h) + V).
  2. (1 + 1) ÷ (5 + 6) = 2 ÷ 11.
  3. ≈ 0.1818, a little lower than 0.2, since mass moved to unseen pairs
Question 8. Decode "they can fish" with the three tag HMM of chapter 5.3.
  1. t = 1: v(N) = 0.7 × 0.3 = 0.21, M and V are 0.
  2. t = 2: v(M) = 0.21 × 0.4 × 1.0 = 0.084, v(V) = 0.21 × 0.4 × 0.3 = 0.0252, v(N) = 0.0084.
  3. t = 3: v(V) = max(0.00336, 0.0756, 0.00756) × 0.7 = 0.05292 from M.
  4. they/N can/M fish/V, P = 0.05292
Question 9. A PCFG gives P(VP → VP PP) = 0.3 and P(NP → NP PP) = 0.2, and the two parses of "I shot an elephant in my pajamas" share every other rule. Which wins?
  1. Shared rules cancel in the ratio.
  2. VP attachment ∝ 0.3 × P(VP → V NP) = 0.3 × 0.7 = 0.21. NP attachment ∝ 0.7 × 0.2 = 0.14.
  3. Ratio 1.5, so VP attachment: I wore the pajamas.

Short notes and compare questions at this level: stemming against lemmatisation (chapter 2.2), backoff against interpolation (chapter 4.3), PP against PP1 in SRILM (chapter 4.4), generative against discriminative taggers (chapter 5.1), Mealy against Moore machines (chapter 5.2), chunks against constituents (chapter 5.4), dependency against phrase structure (chapter 5.5).

Common mistake
State your convention for <s> and </s> before computing. Counting </s> as a predicted token changes both the probability and the N in perplexity.

Question 10. P = (1/2, 1/2), Q = (3/4, 1/4). Find KL(P ‖ Q) in bits.
  1. KL = Σ P log₂(P ÷ Q).
  2. = 0.5 log₂(0.5 ÷ 0.75) + 0.5 log₂(0.5 ÷ 0.25) = 0.5 × (−0.585) + 0.5 × 1.
  3. ≈ 0.2075 bits. Cross entropy H(P, Q) = H(P) + KL = 1.2075 bits
Question 11. P(x, y) = 0.01, P(x) = 0.05 and P(y) = 0.1. Find the pointwise mutual information.
  1. PMI = log₂ P(x, y) ÷ (P(x) P(y)).
  2. P(x) P(y) = 0.005, so the ratio is 2.
  3. PMI = 1 bit: they occur together twice as often as chance
Question 12. A model gives every word of a 1,000 word vocabulary the same probability. What is its perplexity?
  1. P(w) = 1 ÷ 1,000 for every word.
  2. H = log₂ 1,000 ≈ 9.97 bits.
  3. PP = 2H = 1,000
In the exam
Trap options to expect: counting <s> as a predicted token, mixing log base 2 and 10, forgetting to add V in the Laplace denominator, treating columns of an HMM table as summing to 1, and calling a chunk a constituent.
Lesson 5 of 6

Tier 4: derivations, design and critique

Research university exams in the style of MIT, Stanford and Cambridge reward a complete argument more than a final number. State assumptions, derive the result, then say when it fails.

Question 13. Show that the bigram MLE is a relative frequency.

Model answer
  1. For one history h, maximise L = Σw C(h w) log θw subject to Σw θw = 1.
  2. Lagrangian: Σ C(h w) log θw − λ (Σ θw − 1). Setting the derivative to 0 gives C(h w) ÷ θw = λ.
  3. So θw = C(h w) ÷ λ. Summing over w gives λ = Σ C(h w) = C(h).
  4. θw = C(h w) ÷ C(h)
  5. It fails for unseen pairs, which get 0. That is why we smooth.

Question 14. Show that perplexity is 2 to the power of the cross entropy.

Model answer
  1. Per token cross entropy of the test text: H = −(1 ÷ N) log₂ P(w₁ … wN).
  2. Then 2H = 2−(1/N) log₂ P = P−1/N.
  3. That is the definition PP = P−1/N. A perplexity of k means the model is as unsure as a fair choice among k words.
  4. It is only comparable across models with the same vocabulary and tokenisation, since N and the event space must match.

Question 15. Show that a generative tagger and a discriminative tagger choose the same sequence, and prove Viterbi is exact.

Model answer
  1. P(T | W) = P(W, T) ÷ P(W), and P(W) is one positive constant for the fixed sentence, so the argmax is unchanged.
  2. Viterbi: under the Markov assumption, the score after time t depends on the past only through the tag at t.
  3. If a best path used a non best prefix into its tag at t, swapping in the best prefix would raise its score, a contradiction.
  4. So one best score per (t, tag) suffices, costing T q² instead of qT. It breaks when the model is not Markov, for example with features of the whole tag sequence, which is why CRFs with global features need other inference.

Question 16. Show that KL divergence is never negative.

Model answer
  1. −KL(P ‖ Q) = Σ P log(Q ÷ P).
  2. log is concave, so by Jensen's inequality Σ P log(Q ÷ P) ≤ log Σ P (Q ÷ P) = log Σ Q ≤ log 1 = 0.
  3. So KL ≥ 0, with equality only when Q ÷ P is constant, that is P = Q.
  4. Consequence: cross entropy H(P, Q) = H(P) + KL ≥ H(P). No model beats the true entropy on average.

Design and critique questions

A design question such as build a tagger for tweets in Hinglish has no single answer. Structure it as: the data and labels you need, the tagset, the model family and why, how you handle unknown words (suffixes, capitals, subwords), the metric, the error analysis, and the failure cases. A critique question gives an experiment, for example "the new language model has perplexity 40 against 65", and asks what could be wrong: a different vocabulary, a different tokeniser, tuning on the test set, or a test set that overlaps the training data.

In the exam
In derivations, name each assumption when you use it: Markov, output independence, a fixed vocabulary, independent sentences. Examiners often ask what breaks when one fails.
One idea, four depths

At school you count words, read small probabilities and explain ideas with examples.

At university you are given a toy corpus or HMM tables and must compute probabilities, perplexity and the best tag sequence by hand, step by step.

In competitive papers you must recognise the right formula quickly, avoid the traps around <s>, </s>, log bases and smoothing, and round only at the end.

At research universities you derive MLE, perplexity, Viterbi and KL from first principles, state the assumptions and argue when they fail.

Ten mistakes that cost marks

  1. Counting <s> as a predicted token.
  2. Forgetting </s> when the question includes it.
  3. Using V without <unk> when the question adds it, or the other way round.
  4. Mixing log base 2 and log base 10.
  5. Rounding in the middle of a long product.
  6. Summing columns instead of rows when checking an HMM.
  7. Forgetting the start probability in the first Viterbi column.
  8. Reporting the best final cell without tracing the back pointers.
  9. Calling a stem a lemma.
  10. Comparing perplexities across different vocabularies.
